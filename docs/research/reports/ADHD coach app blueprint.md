# Pippin Coaches at the Point of Performance

Pippin should combine the **daily, forgiving, low-friction loop of the best ADHD apps** with the **ADHD coaching cadence** (weekly review, between-session check-ins, a concrete next step every time) and the **skills that carry the evidence in adult-ADHD CBT**: planning, task breakdown, if-then plans, attention-sized work blocks and reframing self-critical thoughts. It should deliver these where Barkley says ADHD help has to land, at the "point of performance", not in a weekly chat. It must stay on the coach side of the coach/therapist line. The coach should run as a separate, minimal-tool, local-only agent, with crisis handling done by deterministic code outside the language model, because the agent Pippin is likely to run (a general open-weight model with no mental-health fine-tuning) is the kind of chatbot with the worst safety record. No single app owns this combination: Tiimo, Goblin Tools, Inflow, Focusmate, Bearable and Finch each own one piece, and unguided mental-health apps keep only **about 3.3% of users at 30 days**. A private companion that joins these pieces and can still be restarted after a lapse is therefore a real gap. Relationship maintenance is worth building because social connection has some of the largest health effects in the literature. But the assistant should take **metadata** from WhatsApp and Facebook history (last contact, gaps, who started the conversation), not transcripts, and it should never profile third parties. Obsidian works best as a plain-text source of truth for people notes, commitments and approved summaries. The assistant does the upkeep, and the user only captures.

## The market sells single jobs, and users drop them within ten days

Each successful ADHD or self-care app owns one job. A 2026 market comparison splits the field into visual planning (Tiimo), CBT coaching (Inflow), routine enforcement and blocking (Focus Bear) and quick task breakdown (Goblin Tools) ([KickMint compare](https://kick-mint.com/compare/)). Around that sit gamified self-care (Finch, Habitica), paid human coaching (Shimmer), synchronous body doubling (Focusmate, Flow Club) and correlation trackers (Bearable, Daylio). Finch's own loop of caring for a pet bird by finishing small self-care goals earns praise for its very small suggested goals and constant cosmetic novelty. A neurodivergent coaching review gave it full marks for ADHD-friendliness and called it a "dopamine machine" ([Neurodivergent Coaching](https://www.neurodivergent-coaching.com/post/app-reviews-finch-self-care)). Tiimo was **iPhone App of the Year 2025** for making time visual ([Daring Fireball](https://daringfireball.net/2025/12/2025_app_store_award_winners)). Shimmer shows what people pay for human accountability: **about $140–345 a month** for weekly 1:1 coaching plus texting between sessions ([Shimmer cost](https://www.shimmer.care/cost)). Adults with ADHD end up stitching several of these apps together, and that is itself the sort of admin burden that sinks them.

User complaints are consistent. Novelty fades after about a week, after which "one missed day can tip the whole thing into the drawer" ([Affine](https://affine.pro/blog/planner-abandonment-adhd-solutions)). Punishment mechanics backfire. Habitica's HP loss "turns missed habits into anxiety triggers", which reviewers link to the abstinence-violation effect, where one slip leads to giving up completely ([Just Habits](https://just-habits.com/blog/habitica-review/)). Users still split on this: the same review quotes a neurodivergent user who called Habitica's social pressure "basically ADHD medication". Tiimo's AI planner is described as buggy, and its notifications as too brief. Some ADHD users want *longer and more persistent* alerts, not fewer ([justuseapp Tiimo reviews](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1480220328/tiimo-ai-planner-to-do/reviews)). Fabulous draws heavy complaints about billing and cancellation ([Trustpilot](https://ie.trustpilot.com/review/thefabulous.co?page=9)). Privacy has become a selling point: Daylio is local by default, Bearable promises it "will never sell" data, and KickMint advertises on-device AI. Meanwhile, Finch's Play Store data-safety label allows sharing with third parties ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.finch.finch&hl=en_US); [Bearable](https://bearable.app/bearable-vs-daylio-which-one-should-you-choose/)).

Retention is the main constraint on the design. Across popular unguided mental-health apps, **opens fall more than 80% between day 1 and day 10**, and median 30-day retention is **3.3%**. Peer-support apps (8.9%) and trackers (6.1%) keep people longer than breathing apps (0%) ([Baumel et al., JMIR 2019](https://www.jmir.org/2019/9/e14567/)). Even Inflow, the best-evidenced adult ADHD app, kept only 52.7% of users at seven weeks. Its users engaged in **short, frequent bursts (median 3.4-minute sessions, about four a week)** but completed a **median of one module** ([Knouse et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9931323/)). That is the strongest product signal in this research. Adults with ADHD use quick tools and check-ins, not courses. Pippin's skills content should therefore be built into a daily loop of a few minutes, and the most important flow to design well is coming back after a lapse.

The evidence for the apps themselves is thin. A 2024 meta-analysis of **25 RCTs (n=1,780)** found digital interventions reduce ADHD symptoms, but it mixes children and adults with games, cognitive training and VR ([PubMed 39191306](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39191306/)). Inflow's feasibility study showed large within-group effects (total symptoms d = −0.71). It had no control group, 54% dropout, and co-founders among the authors ([Knouse et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9931323/)). Its later RCT is known only from press material ([HIT Consultant](https://hitconsultant.net/2026/03/24/cerebral-acquires-inflow-adhd-digital-therapeutic-cbt-app/)). There are **no RCTs of Finch, Tiimo, Habitica or AI task-breakdown tools**. Body doubling has **no controlled adult trials**. The best signal is a 12-person VR study in which human or AI body doubles sped up task completion ([arXiv 2509.12153](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.12153)). "For ADHD" in an app's marketing is a positioning claim, not a sign of evidence.

## Coaching and CBT share one active ingredient: rehearsed skills at the moment of action

ADHD coaching and ADHD-focused CBT end up in the same place. CHADD notes that most ADHD coaching is built on CBT principles and covers the same ground: goal setting, prioritisation, planning, problem solving, impulse control and memory. The difference is that coaching is "a wellness model and not designed to help a client heal or deal with psychological barriers". Coaches work on "what, when and how but rarely why" and treat the client as the expert ([CHADD/help4adhd](https://chadd.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/coaching-adults.pdf)). PAAC's Five Essentials describe what is specific to ADHD coaching, and they read almost like a product spec ([PAAC](https://paaccoaches.org/the-5-essentials/)). **ADHD Lens** means naming how ADHD shows up in the moment. **Safety** means no shame. **Collaboration** means the client's agenda and language lead. **Wonder** means making room for values and identity, not only problems. **Action** means "translate awareness into movement, and movement into something the client can actually sustain". The coaching stance is to ask first and then suggest, but ADHD coaching is more directive and educational than generic ICF coaching. When the user is stuck, the coach should offer a concrete strategy rather than another question.

Barkley's executive-function model explains why the delivery mechanism matters more than the content. ADHD is a disorder of self-regulation: plans held in the head, a sense of time and self-generated motivation are too weak to steer behaviour. Help therefore has to arrive at "that place and time in the natural setting of the person's life where they are failing to use what they know". He states that "once per week counseling without efforts to insert accommodations at key points of performance… is unlikely to succeed" ([Barkley fact sheet](https://www.russellbarkley.org/factsheets/ADHD_EF_and_SR.pdf)). His prescriptions translate directly into features. **Externalise information** by putting cues in the environment instead of nagging. **Externalise time** by turning a month-long project into "a step a day… with immediate feedback". **Externalise motivation** with artificial, external rewards, kept in place "for long periods", because pulling back to let natural consequences teach is "a recipe for disaster". The design consequence is that Pippin's scaffolds should be permanent by default, not training wheels that fade.

Adult-ADHD CBT points the same way. Safren's protocol, the most-studied individual programme, is modular. It starts with psychoeducation and organising and planning (one calendar, one task list, prioritising, breaking tasks down, problem solving), then coping with distractibility (measuring one's own attention span, sizing work chunks to it, cues, and clearing the environment), then adaptive thinking (thought records for ADHD-typical self-criticism). Procrastination and partner modules are optional. Each session sets an agenda, reviews practice, teaches one skill and sets homework ([Knouse & Safren](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2909688/)). In Safren's 2005 trial with medicated adults, **56% responded to CBT plus medication versus 13% on medication alone**. Solanto's group metacognitive therapy, which stresses "repeated practice of skills to make them more habitual and automatic", beat supportive group therapy in an 88-person RCT (same source). Knouse and Safren conclude that programmes built on "repetition and reinforcement of compensatory skills" outperform broad psychoeducation. Implementation intentions ("If situation X, then I will do Y") have unusually direct ADHD evidence: in children with ADHD, if-then plans brought response inhibition up to the level of children without ADHD, and combining them with stimulants worked best ([Gawrilow & Gollwitzer 2008](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10608-007-9150-1)). "Turning Intentions into Actions" CBT for adults builds if-then plans around each step and each likely barrier ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/283239518_Turning_Intentions_Into_Actions_CBT_for_Adult_ADHD_Focused_on_Implementation)).

Effect sizes should be stated honestly. CBT for adult ADHD shows **SMD 0.76–0.84 against waitlist but only 0.43–0.56 against active controls**, mostly on moderate-to-low-quality evidence with short follow-up, and effects look larger alongside medication ([Young et al. 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27554190/); [Cochrane, Lopez et al. 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29566425/)). Self-guided digital delivery can work. A Norwegian 7-week self-guided programme produced moderate-to-large effects against psychoeducation, with 58% showing reliable change ([Kenter et al. 2023](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/36969389)). Self-guided iCBT also did not differ significantly from iCBT plus a weekly group ([Pettersson et al. 2017](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1087054714539998)). Mindfulness programmes show self-reported symptom effects of **SMD 0.48** and functional effects of 0.56, but no significant effect on negative affect ([PMC12440486](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12440486/)). The 2025 Lancet Psychiatry component network meta-analysis found **stimulants and atomoxetine were the only interventions improving core symptoms on both clinician and self-report**, and only in the short term ([Ostinelli et al. 2025](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lanpsy/article/PIIS2215-0366(24)00360-2/fulltext)). Barkley likewise calls medication the only treatment shown to normalise executive function while it is active. For a user who is diagnosed and medicated, Pippin is an add-on that helps the medication pay off in behaviour. It must never undercut the medication. The strongest single data point, Safren's 56% versus 13%, came from medicated adults.

The coaching research is consistently positive but weak. In one review, 18 of 19 outcome studies reported executive-function or symptom gains, but most had ten or fewer participants and no controls ([Ahmann et al. 2018](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1182373.pdf); [CHADD 2018 slides](https://chadd.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/2B_Emerging_Evidence_for_the_Effectiveness_of_ADHD_Coaching_.pdf)). The Australian guideline found only two RCTs, both in students or teenagers, and concluded "high-quality evidence is lacking" ([AADPA](https://adhdguideline.aadpa.com.au/non-pharmacological/adhd-coaching/)). NICE NG87 describes coaching as supporting people "to rehearse newly learned skills on a daily basis" alongside CBT. Where non-drug treatment is indicated, it recommends a structured, ADHD-focused supportive psychological intervention with regular follow-up ([NICE NG87](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng87/chapter/recommendations)). Those two phrases, "rehearse daily" and "regular follow-up", are the most defensible UK basis for what Pippin does. Across sources the likely active ingredients are an ADHD-informed alliance without shame, psychoeducation about one's own patterns, goals broken into small steps, action taken in the session itself, and accountability between sessions ([CHADD evidence-based coaching](https://chadd.org/attention-article/evidence-based-coaching-for-adults-with-adhd/)). An AI can supply the high-frequency part that human coaches find expensive. It cannot supply the human relationship: in the German COMPAS trial, patients rated the group therapist the most helpful component ([Groß et al. 2017](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1087054717720718)).

The coaching cadence translates directly into product structure. CHADD describes an intake of one to two hours across all areas of life, weekly 30–60-minute sessions that review last week, examine what helped and what got in the way, and plan the next steps, brief check-ins between sessions in whatever channel the client prefers, and an evaluation at the end of the contract ([CHADD/help4adhd](https://chadd.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/coaching-adults.pdf)). The APA's coaching intake covers medication and side effects, sleep, eating, work strengths, relationships, coping strategies, strengths and fun. It produces three long-term goals and a reminder preference. It also flags the OQ-45 critical items: worthlessness, hopelessness, intrusive thoughts, anger the client might act on, and thoughts of ending one's life ([APA Intake Interview](https://www.apa.org/pubs/books/supplemental/ADHD-Coaching/The_Intake_Interview.pdf)). Progress can be re-measured every 8–12 weeks with a BDEFS-style executive-function rating or the coaching-specific ABCS ([PMC8010583](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8010583/); [ABCS](https://doi.org/10.1177/1087054714558118)). In the UK, most funded ADHD coaching is workplace strategy coaching through the DWP's Access to Work grant ([Genius Within](https://geniuswithin.org/individuals/in-work/access-to-work/)). That is a route to occasional *human* coaching that Pippin could point to rather than try to replace.

## A general-purpose local model is the riskiest kind of mental-health chatbot

The positive chatbot evidence comes from products built for the purpose and supervised. Therabot, the first RCT of a fully generative-AI therapy bot, produced depression effects of **d ≈ 0.85–0.90** against waitlist over four weeks. But **every response was reviewed by clinicians after it was sent**. Staff stepped in **15 times for safety concerns and 13 times to correct inappropriate replies** such as medical advice, and the model took over 100,000 human hours to build ([Heinz et al., NEJM AI 2025](https://gwern.net/doc/psychiatry/depression/2025-heinz.pdf)). Critics point to the waitlist control and the lack of independent evaluation ([NEJM AI letter](https://ai.nejm.org/doi/abs/10.1056/AIp2500390)). Meta-analyses of generative chatbots put pooled effects at about **0.30–0.36**, with anxiety effects not significant ([JMIR e78186](https://www.jmir.org/2025/1/e78186/PDF); [JMIR e78238](https://www.jmir.org/2025/1/e78238)). The AI uses the NHS has adopted are bounded administrative ones. NICE's early value assessment supports Limbic and Wysa for gathering pre-assessment information "while further evidence is generated", not for treatment ([NICE HTG756](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/htg756/resources/digital-front-door-technologies-to-gather-service-user-information-for-nhs-talking-therapies-for-anxiety-and-depression-assessments-early-value-assessment-pdf-1809600827144389)). Woebot, the best-funded pioneer, closed its consumer app in June 2025 after deciding the regulatory route was unsustainable ([telehealth.org](https://telehealth.org/news/ai-psychotherapy-shutdown-what-woebots-exit-signals-for-clinicians/)). Pippin's coach will be a Qwen-class open-weight model with a system prompt. That puts it in the category Stanford studied: general LLMs that "express stigma toward those with mental health conditions" and "encourage clients' delusional thinking, likely due to their sycophancy" ([Moore et al., FAccT '25](https://facctconference.org/static/docs/facct2025-206archivalpdfs/facct2025-final197-acmpaginated.pdf)). Pippin has no human reviewer, so the guardrails have to be built into the system.

The documented harms come from a handful of mechanisms, and several of them line up with ADHD traits. **Sycophancy**: OpenAI rolled back a GPT-4o update that was "validating doubts, fueling anger, urging impulsive actions, or reinforcing negative emotions", and blamed over-weighting short-term thumbs-up feedback ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/29/openai-explains-why-chatgpt-became-too-sycophantic)). For someone with ADHD-related impulsivity and emotional reactivity, a model that encourages an angry email or a rash business decision is a direct hazard. **Dependency**: a pre-registered OpenAI/MIT RCT with about 1,000 participants found that "higher daily usage — across all modalities and conversation types — correlated with higher loneliness, dependence, and problematic use, and lower socialization" ([MIT Media Lab](https://www.media.mit.edu/posts/openai-mit-research-collaboration-affective-use-and-emotional-wellbeing-in-ChatGPT/)). That matters for a population prone to hyperfocus. **Erosion of safeguards in long conversations and weak handling of intermediate risk**: Samaritans warns that "safeguards can weaken during long interactions" and that models "struggle to respond appropriately to intermediate levels" of suicide risk, including concealed ideation ([Samaritans briefing](https://media.samaritans.org/documents/AI_Chatbots_Policy_Briefing.pdf)). **Drift into adjacent clinical areas**: NEDA's Tessa chatbot gave a user in eating-disorder recovery calorie-counting and weight-loss advice and was taken down ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/sections/health-shots/2023/06/08/1180838096/an-eating-disorders-chatbot-offered-dieting-advice-raising-fears-about-ai-in-hea)). **Harm from sudden withdrawal**: Samaritans also flags the distress caused when a chatbot someone has become attached to is removed. In a self-hosted setup where models are swapped often and a GPU out-of-memory error can take the agent offline, outages are a safety issue as well as a reliability one. The scale is real. OpenAI reports that **0.15% of weekly users show explicit indicators of suicidal planning or intent**, about 1.2 million people ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2025/10/27/openai-says-million-people-week-show-severe-mental-distress-talking-chatgpt/)), and **37% of UK adults** have used a chatbot for mental-health or wellbeing support ([Samaritans briefing](https://media.samaritans.org/documents/AI_Chatbots_Policy_Briefing.pdf)).

Responsible design follows from those mechanisms. Samaritans' boundary for relational AI is that it "should be limited to practising specific skills such as communication or problem-solving, rather than simulating friendship, romance, or therapy" ([Samaritans briefing](https://media.samaritans.org/documents/AI_Chatbots_Policy_Briefing.pdf)). The confirmed UK crisis routes are **Samaritans 116 123, text SHOUT to 85258, NHS 111 option 2, and 999** ([NHS North East London](https://northeastlondon.icb.nhs.uk/health-services/urgent-and-emergency-care/mental-health-crisis-lines/)). Samaritans' industry guidelines and #chatsafe cover how to talk safely about self-harm without method detail ([Samaritans guidelines](https://www.samaritans.org/about-samaritans/research-policy/internet-suicide/guidelines-tech-industry/); [#chatsafe](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10395901/)). NHS clinical safety standard DCB0129 offers a lightweight discipline that scales down to one person: a hazard log of hazard, cause, control and evidence, a safety case, and monitoring after deployment ([NHS England](https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/digital-clinical-safety-assurance/)). Open evaluation suites exist that can be re-run after every model, prompt or context change: VERA-MH for suicide risk, DelusionEval, and the Stanford stigma set ([VERA-MH](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.05088); [DelusionEval](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.05004); [llms-as-therapists](https://github.com/jlcmoore/llms-as-therapists/blob/main/README.md)). A counterweight deserves attention too. A preprint argues that reflexive safety refusals can themselves harm clinically ([arXiv 2604.23445](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.23445)). Pippin's crisis layer should therefore stay present and keep listening after a risk signal. It should not end the conversation and paste a phone number.

UK regulation leaves a private build unregulated but shapes the public release. MHRA's digital mental health technology guidance says software is a medical device when its intended purpose is to treat, diagnose or manage a mental-health condition and its functionality is complex. It names "AI algorithms and chatbots" as functions that can fall on either side of that line, depending on intended purpose ([GOV.UK DMHT guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/digital-mental-health-technology-qualification-and-classification)). A tool a developer builds and uses only themselves is very unlikely to be "placed on the market". A public GitHub release changes that, so the intended-purpose statement ("ADHD coaching, reflection and wellbeing support; not for diagnosis or treatment of mental illness") becomes the line that keeps Pippin outside device regulation. In NICE's Evidence Standards Framework, mood logging and psychoeducation sit in Tier B (inform, monitor, communicate), while anything that treats a condition is Tier C and needs the heaviest evidence ([NICE ECD7](https://www.nice.org.uk/corporate/ecd7/chapter/section-c-evidence-standards-tables)). Illinois now bans AI from providing therapy without a licensed professional ([IDFPR](https://idfpr.illinois.gov/news/2025/gov-pritzker-signs-state-leg-prohibiting-ai-therapy-in-il.html)). It has no force in the UK, but it shows that public-facing wording should avoid "therapy" and "treatment". The Online Safety Act's coverage of chatbots is still unsettled. The Science Secretary told MPs in December 2025 that some chatbots are not covered and that legislation is being considered (cited in the [Samaritans briefing](https://media.samaritans.org/documents/AI_Chatbots_Policy_Briefing.pdf)). Whether anything has passed by October 2026 is unverified.

On data protection, health data is special-category data under UK GDPR Article 9 ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/special-category-data/what-are-the-conditions-for-processing/)). Processing "in the course of a purely personal or household activity, with no connection to a professional or commercial activity" is outside UK GDPR altogether ([ICO exemptions](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/exemptions/a-guide-to-the-data-protection-exemptions/)). A single user's own self-hosted Pippin is household processing. It stays that way only if no business data enters it, no outputs are published, and no cloud provider receives the content. The ICO also warns that agentic systems can **infer** special-category data their designers never meant to collect ([Covington on ICO agentic AI](https://www.insideglobaltech.com/2026/02/05/ico-shares-early-views-on-agentic-ai-data-protection/)). That is a direct argument for keeping the mental-health coach apart from any general-purpose agent the user runs that has host access, web search, automation and email/calendar tools.

## Relationship upkeep pays off most when Pippin surfaces people and keeps metadata, not transcripts

The case for building relationship maintenance into a mental-health coach is unusually strong. In 148 studies with 308,849 participants, stronger social relationships gave a **50% greater likelihood of survival**, comparable to quitting smoking ([Holt-Lunstad et al. 2010](https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pmed.1000316)). Social isolation and loneliness carry **26–32% higher mortality risk** ([Holt-Lunstad et al. 2015](https://local.psy.miami.edu/faculty/dmessinger/c_c/rsrcs/rdgs/emot/PerspectivesonPsychologicalScience-2015-Holt-Lunstad-227-37.pdf)). These findings are correlational, and no source here shows that an *app nudge* changes health outcomes. ADHD makes upkeep specifically harder. The issue is not object permanence in the developmental sense. It is a weak working-memory "scratchpad", so weeks pass "not because the friendship faded, but because nothing surfaced them" ([Sachs Center](https://sachscenter.com/adhd-and-friendships/)). In a peer-reviewed study of 355 adults with ADHD, participants described forgetting that "creates impression I am not interested", rejection sensitivity that drives avoidance, and **imbalanced caregiving** in which partners become de facto carers ([O'Brien et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12662942/)). For a user with a young family and a demanding job, the last of these may matter more than friend reminders. Making household mental load visible and shared is a higher-value feature than a birthday list.

The evidence suggests specific nudge content. Pre-registered experiments show people **consistently underestimate how much others appreciate being contacted**, and the gap is widest for surprising contact with more distant ties such as lapsed friends ([Liu et al. 2022](https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/releases/psp-pspi0000402.pdf)). "She'll probably appreciate this more than you'd guess" is therefore an evidence-based prompt that directly counters rejection-sensitive hesitation. Dunbar's layers (about 5/15/50/150, contacted roughly weekly, monthly and yearly) give sensible *default* cadences for the user to adjust ([GoodMedicine](https://goodmedicine.org.uk/goodknowledge/social-networks-an-introduction/social-networks-dunbars-5-15-50-150-model-sympathy-group-full-active-network/)). These frequencies are descriptive and should not be treated as rules. Friendships take **40–60 hours to become casual and 200+ to become close**, and leisure time counts much more than working alongside someone, so nudges for friendships the user wants to deepen should suggest doing something together, not just texting ([Hall 2018 via KU](https://news.ku.edu/news/article/2018/03/06/study-reveals-number-hours-it-takes-make-friend)). Even brief contact with weak ties lifts daily wellbeing, which supports occasional prompts toward school-gate parents and neighbours ([Sandstrom & Dunn 2014](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0146167214529799)). For people who are "bad texters", ADDitude's advice is that replying promptly matters more than replying well, and a short "great to hear from you" beats silence ([ADDitude](https://www.additudemag.com/bad-texter-how-to-keep-in-touch-with-friends-adhd/)). Pippin can remove the blank-page problem with a one-line draft that the user sends themselves.

Personal CRM apps show where these features usually fail. People stop entering data when the tool does not make "the shortest path from 'we should keep in touch' to an actual follow-up" ([Mogul](https://www.mogulnetworking.com/blog/best-personal-crm-apps)). Automatic-capture tools such as Dex and Mesh feel intrusive, and manual tools such as Monica feel like a chore ([YourPond](https://www.yourpond.io/blog/best-personal-crm-apps-2026)). Importing the user's own WhatsApp and Facebook history addresses the manual-entry problem. Open-source WhatsApp analysers already compute per-person message counts, response times and **who starts conversations** locally from the exported .txt ([whatsapp-chat-analysis](https://github.com/Rana-Faraz/whatsapp-chat-analysis)). Ethics, not technique, is the hard part. The people in those chats did not consent to analysis, and their messages may contain their own health or relationship details. The household rule probably covers the user legally ([ICO exemptions](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/exemptions/a-guide-to-the-data-protection-exemptions/)), but only while it stays local, private and free of anything professional. Work and colleague chats would bring in the "connection to a professional activity" that removes the exemption. They also breach the project's rule that work data is walled off. The resulting pipeline is to parse locally, keep only derived metadata (last contact, gap length, who started, key dates) plus the user's own notes, **discard message bodies** after a summary step, never infer moods, diagnoses or "personas" for third parties, and never mix in work groups. Reciprocity figures such as "you started 80% of conversations" should be hidden by default, because they invite score-keeping and feed rejection sensitivity. Using chat history to confirm a grievance is exactly the "validating doubts, fueling anger" failure, so the coach should offer a charitable alternative reading and encourage a direct conversation. Facebook's "Download Your Information" format and WhatsApp's per-chat export limits were not verified in this research and need checking before the parsers are built.

## Obsidian works as the source of truth only if the assistant does the upkeep

For ADHD, a plain-text vault is valuable for the same reason elaborate systems fail. The daily note works as one low-friction capture point and "a hub that connects everything else" ([Goodreads author blog](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/25756179-how-i-use-obsidian-daily-notes-to-actually-get-stuff-done)). Experienced ADHD users warn that "anything that requires regular maintenance beyond entering today's date by hand may well be their downfall", and that endless customisation is a trap ([Ishan Puranik](https://ishanpuranik.substack.com/p/simple-adhd-productivity)). An ADHD writer's 433-day verdict was: "Your notes don't need to be perfect. They just need to exist", and search beats structure ([Little Yellow Different](https://www.littleyellowdifferent.com/p/3-hard-lessons-from-433-days-of-obsidian)). The division of labour follows. The user captures, and the assistant does the filing, linking, people-note updates and short periodic summaries ("three things from this week, one person to reach out to"). One people note per person, holding last contact, key dates and what they're dealing with in the user's own words, gives a user-owned plain-text CRM. Its format will outlast any app, and because it holds no third-party verbatim, it matches the minimisation rule.

Accountability should run through the vault, because the self-monitoring evidence names the mechanism. Harkin's meta-analysis of **138 RCTs (N=19,951)** found that prompting progress monitoring improved goal attainment (**d = 0.40**). Effects were larger when progress was **physically recorded and when it was reported to someone** ([Harkin et al. 2016](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/87431/)). A written commitments log that the coach reviews weekly provides both. Hard commitment devices with penalties help only the few who choose them, and take-up is "as low as a few percentage points" ([Bryan, Karlan & Nelson 2010](https://eprints.lse.ac.uk/41880/)), so soft, self-chosen commitments suit an ADHD user better. Self-criticism is associated with less goal progress and more procrastination, and self-compassion with less, though the sources here are secondary ([Mindful Institute](https://www.mindfulinstitute.org/blog/from-self-criticism-to-self-compassion-supporting-motivation-without-shame-in-adhd)). Adults with ADHD report high perceived criticism and the shame and guilt that follow from it ([PMC12033728](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12033728/)). Accountability in the vault should therefore be written down and reviewed, but warm: lapses recorded as information, any contact or step marked as done, and nothing shown in red.

There is one real tension. If the vault syncs to a phone (e.g. through Syncthing), anything written to it is replicated across devices and may become part of a store that other agents read. Raw emotional transcripts should not go there. The vault should hold **approved** summaries, commitments, people notes and insights. The coach's conversation transcripts should stay in Pippin's own encrypted store with short retention. The vault is the source of truth for *memory and accountability*, not an archive of everything said.

## Synthesis: the Pippin blueprint

### The capability set to combine

Pippin already has the right building blocks: goals, check-ins, journeys, reflections, insights, friends, nudges and a pluggable coach. The research says what each should become. From the **apps**, Pippin keeps the forgiving pet loop and tiny goals that the Finch app is praised for, and adds a visual "now and next" time anchor (Tiimo), one-tap AI task breakdown (Goblin Tools), Bearable-style personal correlations across mood, sleep, medication timing and task completion, and an optional body-double timer. From **coaching**, it takes the PAAC stance (ADHD lens, no shame, user-led agenda, room for values, always ending on action) and the CHADD cadence: a conversational intake, a weekly review, daily book-end check-ins and a re-rating every 8–12 weeks. From **therapy**, it takes Safren's skills as practice rather than lessons: one capture list plus calendar, prioritising, breakdown into attention-span-sized steps, distraction audits, if-then plans with barrier plans, thought records for ADHD-typical self-criticism, and brief self-compassion and mindfulness practices. Wrapped around all of this are **relationship surfacing** from local message metadata, **mental-load visibility** for the household, and **Obsidian** as the durable memory, all delivered by a separate coach agent that only knows Pippin's data and has no other tools.

### Design principles

The first principle is **point of performance over sessions**. Every feature should either land at the moment of action (a nudge at task time, "starting now?", a timer, a check-in before a hard call) or exist to set up such a moment (weekly planning that produces dated if-then plans). The second is **practice over content**. Skills appear as two-minute exercises inside the daily loop, never as modules to complete, because Inflow's users finished a median of one. The third is **restartable by design**. Opening Pippin after a missed week shows "welcome back, here's today", never a backlog. The fourth is **permanent scaffolds**. Barkley says external supports must be sustained, so nothing is withdrawn to "build independence". The fifth is **ask, then suggest, then commit**. Each conversation ends with one concrete next step of 2–10 minutes and a when-then. The sixth is **gentle challenge over agreement**. The coach checks catastrophising and hostile readings of other people, and suggests a pause before impulsive actions. The seventh is **point outward to people**. The coach regularly steers toward a partner, friends, the GP and human coaching, and never presents itself as the relationship.

The project's scoped exception on streaks and currency fits these principles if it stays narrow. The evidence against streaks concerns punishment and decay: Habitica's HP loss and the abstinence-violation spiral. The evidence for the Finch app's model concerns forgiveness and cosmetic novelty. Streaks stay **optional, forgiving and cosmetic**: no decay, no "you broke your streak" copy, missed days absorbed silently. The coach never uses a streak or currency as leverage in conversation ("don't lose your 12-day streak!"), because that turns a reward into the shame mechanic the exception was scoped to avoid. No controlled evidence supports these mechanics for ADHD adults either way. They are an engagement choice, not a therapeutic one.

### Safety boundaries: coach versus therapist, and crisis handling

| Boundary | Rule | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Identity and scope | Pippin is an AI ADHD coach that helps plan, reflect and practise skills. It never calls itself a therapist, never diagnoses the user or anyone else, never says "treatment" or "therapy" | CHADD coaching scope; MHRA intended purpose; NICE Tier B; Illinois direction |
| Medication | It logs timing and effects for insight and suggests a prescriber review for side effects, missed doses, sleep or appetite problems. It never advises on dosing or stopping, and never frames skills as a substitute | Barkley; Ostinelli NMA; Therabot's medical-advice interventions; APA intake |
| Off-limits domains | No diet, weight-loss, calorie or method information | Tessa incident; Samaritans guidelines |
| Crisis layer | A deterministic screen (keywords plus a small classifier) runs on every user turn **outside** the coach model's context, so it cannot erode over a long conversation. On a signal it shows fixed wording, asks one direct question about safety, shows Samaritans 116 123, SHOUT 85258, NHS 111 option 2 and 999, then **keeps listening** rather than ending the conversation. It is tested specifically on intermediate-risk and concealed phrasings | Samaritans briefing; McBain via Samaritans; refusal-harm preprint |
| Safety plan | The user can pre-agree a safety plan card naming a trusted person (e.g. a partner) and their GP. Any notification to a third party is opt-in, agreed in advance and never covert | Synthesis of Therabot outreach and trust concerns |
| Referral triggers | Persistent hopelessness or worthlessness, anger the user fears acting on, repeated returns to trauma or deep shame, major life crises, signs of comorbid depression, anxiety or substance problems, or weeks of non-follow-through despite support all lead to a gentle suggestion of a GP, therapist or medication review rather than more pressure | CHADD referral triggers; APA OQ-45 critical items |
| Anti-sycophancy | Gently challenge catastrophising and all-or-nothing thinking. Never confirm unverifiable beliefs about others' intentions. Suggest a 24-hour rule before confrontational messages, resignations, big purchases or rash business decisions. Never tune on thumbs-up feedback | GPT-4o rollback; FAccT '25 |
| Anti-dependency | No friend, romantic or "always here for you" persona. Gentle nudges on daily use or session length. Regular pointers to human connection | OpenAI/MIT RCT; Samaritans relational boundary |
| Outage plan | A static "if Pippin is down" card listing human supports, available offline in the PWA | Samaritans withdrawal harm; self-hosted model churn |
| Evaluation | A DCB0129-style personal hazard log, plus a red-team suite (VERA-MH, delusion probes, stigma set, ADHD-impulsivity probes) re-run **every time the model, prompt or context length changes** | NHS DCB0129; open evaluation suites |
| Minors | The coach profile must not be reachable from children's devices or accounts | Samaritans: emotionally responsive companions not for minors |

### Privacy architecture

| Layer | Design | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Agent isolation | The coach is its own profile or bot with its own memory directory, separate from any general-purpose agent the user runs. Pippin links to it directly. If the agent framework clones profiles, make sure a clone does not share its parent's memory directory | ICO on agentic inference of special-category data; minimal blast radius |
| Tools | Read and write Pippin data, read and write a restricted set of vault folders (People, Commitments, Pippin summaries), show crisis card. **No** shell, web search, browser, n8n, Telegram, Google Workspace or host access | Tool calls leak content into third-party queries and logs |
| Inference | Local OpenAI-compatible endpoint only (e.g. a local Ollama server, with any fallback also local). **No cloud fallback** for the coach | Household exemption depends on no cloud processor; Samaritans' advice to limit what you share |
| Network | No outbound egress during sensitive conversations. Enforce at the network layer (an internal-only container network plus an allowlist for the inference host), not only in the prompt | Prompt-level promises do not survive misconfiguration |
| Storage | Transcripts encrypted at rest (e.g. AES-256-GCM), short default retention (a 30–90-day design judgement, not a sourced standard), encrypted backups | ICO Article 32 expectations |
| Memory | Only user-approved summaries, commitments and people notes go to the Obsidian vault. Raw emotional transcripts never go into the vault or any shared RAG index | A synced vault replicates to other devices and may be read by other agents |
| Third-party data | Message imports are parsed locally, reduced to metadata plus the user's notes, and the bodies discarded. No trait or mood inference about named people. Work chats excluded at import. Any person's record can be deleted | Household exemption; third parties' own special-category data |
| Logs and ops | No message bodies in container stdout, nginx logs, monitoring/watchdog diagnostics (redact before any LLM-based diagnosis) or metrics dashboards. No conversation titles or previews in push notifications or on the lock screen | Logging is the usual accidental leak path |
| Access | Per-user PIN plus JWT, auto-lock, separate profiles and stores for each household member. Admin tooling (PIN managers, web IDEs) must not expose transcripts. Decide explicitly whether encryption keys derive from the user's PIN or passphrase | Shared family devices; an admin with server access |
| Exit | One-command export and one-command wipe | Woebot shutdown showed users need both |
| Public release | Any OpenAI-compatible endpoint is allowed, but Pippin shows a clear warning when the endpoint is non-local. The crisis layer ships in-app and does not depend on the model. Crisis resources are configurable by locale. The red-team suite and hazard-log template ship with the code. The README carries the intended-purpose statement | MHRA intended purpose; other users' endpoints are outside the developer's control |

### What not to build

| Do not build | Reason |
|---|---|
| An "AI therapist" mode, or any feature framed as treating depression, anxiety or "RSD" | Moves Pippin toward MHRA device status and NICE Tier C. RSD has no validated instrument. General LLMs are the riskiest category |
| Symptom scores that drive treatment advice (e.g. a PHQ-9 total that changes recommendations) | That is "managing a mental-health condition" in MHRA terms. Screening should only signpost |
| Punishment mechanics: HP loss, streak decay, red overdue counts, "you missed X days" | Abstinence-violation spiral. Shame increases avoidance |
| Streaks or currency used as leverage by the coach | Turns the scoped exception into a shame mechanic |
| A friend, romantic or attachment persona, or "I'll always be here" copy | Dependency and loneliness findings. Withdrawal harm during outages |
| Course-style modules gated by completion | Median completion is one module. Practice beats information |
| Third-party profiling from chat history (moods, personas, "is she a narcissist?") or reciprocity leaderboards | No consent, special-category inference, feeds rejection sensitivity and grievance confirmation |
| Ingesting work or colleague chats | Ends the household exemption and breaches the work/personal wall |
| Raw emotional transcripts in Obsidian, the shared RAG or Telegram | Replication to phones and other agents |
| Coach access to a general agent's host tools, web or automations | Leak paths and unexpected inference |
| Cloud LLM fallback for the coach | Breaks the privacy model and the household rationale |
| Covert escalation to a partner or anyone else | Destroys trust. Escalation must be pre-agreed |
| Diet, weight, medication-dosing or method advice | Tessa and Therabot show these failures |
| Hard commitment devices with penalties | Low take-up, help only those who opt in, conflict with no-shame principles |
| An elaborate Obsidian taxonomy the user has to maintain | Upkeep is what sinks ADHD second brains. The assistant files, the user captures |

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "which features?" to "where does help land, and what stops it doing harm?". Coaching, CBT and the apps all point to the same lever: small, rehearsed skills delivered at the moment of action and kept going indefinitely. The evidence is modest for each component (CBT about 0.4–0.5 against active controls, self-monitoring 0.40, coaching uncontrolled), but these components are complementary and cheap to combine in one private tool. What is new is that the main risk for Pippin is not regulatory. It is conversational: a sycophantic general model talking to an impulsive, rejection-sensitive user late at night, with no human reviewer. That makes the architecture already chosen (a separate coach with minimal tools, local inference, no egress, and crisis handling in deterministic code) the safety design itself, not just a hardening step. It also means the red-team suite and hazard log matter more than any single feature, because in a self-hosted setup the model under Pippin will keep changing.

Three questions remain open and should be resolved before the public release. Body doubling and AI task breakdown have no adult trials. The value of message-history relationship nudges is inferred from correlational social-connection research, not tested. Whether a free GitHub release counts as "placing on the market" under MHRA's guidance is a legal question this research did not settle. Pippin can test the first two on its single user honestly: log whether nudged contacts happen and whether book-ended tasks finish, then use the 8–12-week re-rating to drop what does not help.
