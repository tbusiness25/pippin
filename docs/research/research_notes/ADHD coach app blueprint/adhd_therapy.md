# Evidence-based psychological therapy for adult ADHD (for a self-help companion app)

Research date: 2026-10-01. Researcher hit the ~15-tool-call limit; NICE and Lancet pages returned HTTP 403, so some NICE/Lancet detail comes from search snippets and secondary summaries. Items marked "UNVERIFIED (background knowledge)" were not confirmed by a source in this session. The report writer should not state them as fact without hedging.

## 1. CBT protocols: Safren ("Mastering Your Adult ADHD"), Solanto meta-cognitive therapy, Young & Bramham, Knouse

### Takeaway
The best-studied individual protocol (Safren) is a modular, skills-first CBT: psychoeducation, then organising and planning (calendar plus a single task list, prioritising, breaking tasks down, problem-solving), coping with distractibility, and adaptive thinking (thought records), with optional procrastination and partner modules. Weekly homework and a fixed session structure run throughout. Solanto's group meta-cognitive therapy targets time management, organisation and planning, with repeated practice to make skills automatic. Across protocols, the active ingredient appears to be repeated rehearsal of compensatory skills, not psychoeducation alone. That is the part an app can most plausibly support.

### Cited Findings
- Safren programme: three core modules recommended for all clients: (1) psychoeducation and organising/planning, (2) coping with distractibility, (3) adaptive thinking. Optional modules: (4) procrastination, (5) involving a partner or spouse. — [PMC2909688, Knouse & Safren "Current Status of CBT for Adult ADHD"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2909688/); [OUP therapist guide](https://academic.oup.com/book/1240)
- Organising and planning module: a simple calendar and task-list system, prioritisation strategies, breaking overwhelming tasks into manageable pieces, problem-solving skills. Psychoeducation and motivation exercises open this module. — [PMC2909688](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2909688/)
- Distractibility module: becoming aware of one's own attention span, breaking tasks into chunks that fit that span, visual and auditory reminders to cue on-task behaviour, and reducing distractions in the physical environment. — [PMC2909688](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2909688/)
- Adaptive thinking module: cognitive restructuring adapted for ADHD. This covers thought records, identifying cognitive errors and writing rational responses. — [PMC2909688](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2909688/)
- Format: 12–15 individual sessions. Each session sets an agenda, reviews symptoms and adherence, reviews and assigns homework, and reinforces skills. An anger/frustration module was trialled and then dropped as overwhelming. — [PMC2909688](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2909688/)
- Safren 2005 RCT (n=31, medicated adults): CBT plus medication beat medication alone. Responders were 56% for CBT vs 13% for controls; effect size on independent-assessor-rated symptoms was 1.74. — [PMC2909688](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2909688/)
- Solanto meta-cognitive therapy (MCT): 10 weekly 2-hour group sessions of 5–8 people. Covers time management, behavioural activation, procrastination strategies, organisation and planning, with an emphasis on "repeated practice of skills to make them more habitual and automatic". Each session reviews at-home application, takes peer feedback, teaches a new skill and sets homework. — [PMC2909688](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2909688/)
- Solanto 2008 open trial (n=30): 47% fell below the clinical cutoff for inattentive symptoms; effect size on total symptoms was 1.57. Solanto 2010 RCT (n=88): MCT beat supportive group psychotherapy on self-reported and clinician-rated inattention, and more participants met responder criteria (≥30% reduction). — [PMC2909688](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2909688/)
- Knouse & Safren's synthesis: treatments that emphasise "repetition and reinforcement of compensatory skills" aimed at core symptoms produced larger effects than broad psychoeducational approaches. — [PMC2909688](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2909688/)
- Knouse et al. 2017 meta-analysis of cognitive-behavioural treatments for adult ADHD (existence confirmed; effect sizes not retrieved). — [PubMed 28504540](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28504540/)

### Inferences
- The app can borrow almost directly: one capture list plus calendar, a prioritising routine (e.g. A/B/C), task breakdown into steps that fit the user's measured attention span, a timer for the attention span plus a planned break, cue/reminder setup, distraction audits of the environment, and a digital thought record.
- The session structure maps onto a weekly check-in loop: agenda, review of last week's practice, one new skill, assign practice. That is the Safren/Solanto backbone.
- The "repeated practice beats information" finding argues for designing around daily practice and if-then plans rather than reading content.

### Gaps
- Exact session-by-session table of contents for the 2nd edition of Safren's client workbook (2017) was not retrieved (Scribd/OUP pages only). UNVERIFIED (background knowledge): the 2nd edition adds an optional module on applying skills to procrastination, and the partner session is placed early.
- Solanto's specific mottos ("If it's not in the planner it doesn't exist" etc.) were not confirmed.
- Young & Bramham's R&R2ADHD (Reasoning & Rehabilitation, 15 sessions; modules on attention, problem-solving, emotional control, social skills and critical reasoning) was not sourced in this session. UNVERIFIED (background knowledge). It has a UK pedigree, from Susan Young.
- Safren 2010 JAMA RCT (n=86, CBT vs relaxation plus educational support in medicated adults; CBT superior) was not verified here. UNVERIFIED (background knowledge).

## 2. DBT-based skills groups (Hesslinger, Philipsen/COMPAS), mindfulness (Zylowska MAPs), ACT

### Takeaway
DBT-adapted group programmes (Hesslinger/Philipsen) were tested at scale in the German COMPAS trial (n=419). Patients rated the ADHD-specific group as more helpful, and improvements held at 1.5-year follow-up. On the primary symptom outcome, however, the structured group was not clearly superior to individual clinical management (see Gaps). Mindfulness programmes (MAPs, MBCT-based) show small-to-moderate effects on ADHD symptoms and function, with inconclusive effects on emotion.

### Cited Findings
- COMPAS: multicentre RCT with a 2×2 factorial design. Arms were cognitive-behavioural group psychotherapy (GPT, the Hesslinger/Philipsen DBT-derived programme) vs individual clinical management (CM), each combined with methylphenidate vs placebo, over 52 weeks. Full analysis set n=419. — [Groß et al., J Atten Disord / PubMed 28745107](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28745107/); [JAMA Psychiatry RCT record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/283499501_Effects_of_Group_Psychotherapy_Individual_Counseling_Methylphenidate_and_Placebo_in_the_Treatment_of_Adult_Attention-DeficitHyperactivity_Disorder_A_Randomized_Clinical_Trial)
- In COMPAS, patients' subjective ratings favoured ADHD-specific group psychotherapy over non-specific management, both post-treatment and at follow-up. The group therapist was rated the most helpful component. Attendance was 78–94% of sessions. — [Groß et al. 2017](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1087054717720718)
- In COMPAS, quality of life improved and stayed improved long-term regardless of methylphenidate vs placebo and regardless of GPT vs CM. — [Long-term QoL, COMPAS](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00207411.2021.1910172)
- Symptom improvement was maintained 1.5 years after the 52-week treatment period. — [PubMed 31150084](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31150084/)
- A 2025 meta-analysis of DBT RCTs for adult ADHD reported moderate reductions in ADHD symptoms and improved quality of life vs controls (details not retrieved). — [Psychiatry Research 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0165032725025492)
- MAPs (Zylowska): aims to build self-regulation of attention, emotion and behaviour; awareness of ADHD challenges; self-acceptance; and self-compassion. Mitchell et al. 2017 ran the first controlled pilot in adults only (J Atten Disord 21(13):1105–1120); it was feasible and acceptable and measured core symptoms, executive function and emotion dysregulation. — [Guilford clinician's guide](https://www.guilford.com/books/Mindfulness-for-Adult-ADHD/Zylowska-Mitchell/9781462545001); [PMC6476147 systematic review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6476147)
- Meta-analysis of mindfulness-based interventions (MBIs) for adult ADHD: 10 controlled trials, more than 600 participants, programmes of 6–13 weeks mostly derived from MBCT/MBSR, with homework. Results: self-reported ADHD symptoms SMD 0.48; observer-rated SMD 0.32; functional outcomes SMD 0.56. Negative affect (SMD 0.31) and quality of life were not significant. High heterogeneity (I² 69–83%) and risk-of-bias issues. — [PMC12440486](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12440486/)
- Ostinelli et al. 2025 component network meta-analysis (Lancet Psychiatry 12:32–43): MBCT and psychoeducation showed favourable effects on core symptoms, but psychoeducation rested on only three studies. Only stimulants and atomoxetine reduced core symptoms on both clinician and self-report scales. Benefits were short-term, and no medication improved quality of life. — [Lancet Psychiatry](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lanpsy/article/PIIS2215-0366(24)00360-2/fulltext); [PubMed abstract via PRIME](https://www.unboundmedicine.com/medline/citation/39701638/); [SMC expert reaction](https://www.sciencemediacentre.org/expert-reaction-to-network-meta-analysis-of-pharmacological-psychological-and-neurostimulatory-interventions-for-adhd/)

### Inferences
- DBT-group content (mindfulness, emotion regulation, distress tolerance, behaviour analysis, chaos/control, stress management) is mostly skills and handouts, so it suits self-help well. However, COMPAS suggests the structure and regular contact may matter as much as the specific content.
- Short guided mindfulness practices (attention anchoring, noting distraction, "STOP" pauses, self-compassion) are reasonable app features. Claims should be modest: small-to-moderate effects on symptoms, unproven effects on mood.

### Gaps
- COMPAS primary outcome (Philipsen et al., JAMA Psychiatry 2015): UNVERIFIED (background knowledge). GPT was not superior to CM on the observer-rated ADHD index, while methylphenidate beat placebo. Confirm before stating.
- The Hesslinger 2002 programme outline (13 sessions) was not retrieved.
- No adult-ADHD-specific ACT RCT data were found in this session.

## 3. Emotional dysregulation and "rejection-sensitive dysphoria"

### Takeaway
Emotional dysregulation in adult ADHD is well documented, with emotional lability showing the strongest effect. Rejection-sensitive dysphoria (RSD) is a clinical/popular concept with no validated instrument and no DSM status. Therapists address emotion via CBT (individual CBT appears best for dysregulation), DBT skills and mindfulness. An app should talk about "emotional reactivity" or "rejection sensitivity" rather than present RSD as a diagnosis.

### Cited Findings
- Meta-analysis: adults with ADHD show significantly higher general emotional dysregulation than controls; emotional lability has the strongest effect. — [PMC7069054](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7069054/)
- A systematic review argues that emotion dysregulation is a core symptom of adult ADHD. — [PLOS ONE 2023](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0280131)
- Individual CBT was reported to better address emotional dysregulation and quality of life (search summary of a 2025 CBT meta-analysis; details not retrieved). — [J Affect Disord 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0165032725025492)
- RSD is not a DSM-5 diagnosis. It was introduced from clinical experience, not peer-reviewed research. Rejection sensitivity as a trait has a validated measure and a large literature, but "RSD" has no validated instrument. Circulating prevalence figures (30–99%) cannot be traced to primary sources. — [Psychology Today, 2026](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/if-i-be-waspish/202604/rejection-sensitivity-dysphoria-the-actual-research); [Baltimore Therapy Group review](https://www.therapistsinbaltimore.com/therapy-insights-blog/2025/12/20/rejection-sensitive-dysphoria-rsd-what-research-actually-shows) (secondary sources)
- Qualitative work on lived experience of rejection sensitivity in ADHD exists. — [PLOS ONE qualitative study](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0314669)
- Some quantitative studies did not find elevated rejection sensitivity in ADHD (e.g. a study of undergraduate men). — [Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/if-i-be-waspish/202604/rejection-sensitivity-dysphoria-the-actual-research)

### Inferences
- App content for emotion can safely include naming and labelling feelings, a pause/urge-surf routine, thought records for "they're angry with me" interpretations, self-compassion, and a post-event review. It should avoid diagnosing RSD and avoid promising medication-like relief.

### Gaps
- No RCT specifically targeting rejection sensitivity in adult ADHD was found.

## 4. Comorbid anxiety/depression, low self-esteem, shame

### Takeaway
CBT for adult ADHD produces low-quality-evidence improvements in depression and anxiety (Cochrane 2018). Adaptive-thinking modules target the self-critical, catastrophic thinking built up over years of underperformance. Mindfulness has not clearly improved affect. Moderate-to-severe depression, anxiety or suicidality need clinician care.

### Cited Findings
- Cochrane (Lopez et al. 2018): low-quality evidence that CBT may improve common secondary problems such as depression and anxiety. — [PubMed 29566425](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29566425/)
- Liu et al. 2023 meta-analysis: the effectiveness of CBT-based interventions for adults with ADHD "extends beyond core symptoms" (title-level finding; effect sizes not retrieved). — [Psychol Psychother 2023](https://bpspsychub.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/papt.12455)
- MBIs: the effect on negative affect was not significant (SMD 0.31). — [PMC12440486](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12440486/)
- Safren's adaptive-thinking module adapts cognitive restructuring for ADHD. — [PMC2909688](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2909688/)

### Inferences
- A self-help app should screen and signpost (e.g. PHQ-9/GAD-7-style check-ins with crisis routing to NHS 111 option 2 / Samaritans). It should not try to treat comorbid depression.
- Shame-reducing framing (ADHD as a brain-based difference; separating the person from the "system failure") aligns with psychoeducation and self-compassion components.

### Gaps
- No specific trial data were found on self-esteem/shame outcomes in adult ADHD CBT.

## 5. Couples/relationship work (Orlov, Tuckman, Pera) and family impact

### Takeaway
Relationship strain in ADHD couples is well described, but the treatment evidence is very thin. Essentially one empirical treatment study exists (Wymbs & Molina 2015, integrative couples group). Orlov/Tuckman/Pera are practitioner books, not tested protocols. Safren's optional partner module is the only partner component inside an RCT-tested CBT package.

### Cited Findings
- Only one empirical study (Wymbs & Molina 2015) evaluated treatment of adult ADHD in a couples context. It combined CBT-for-ADHD components with an evidence-based relationship-distress prevention programme. — [Wymbs & Molina 2015, Cogn Behav Pract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1077722914001205)
- Components of that integrative work: psychoeducation, relationship-skills practice, learning to distinguish, accept and tolerate unchangeable ADHD-related behaviours, and reframing differences as strengths. — [search summary of review literature; see Wymbs & Molina](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1077722914001205)
- Couples with an ADHD partner report more anxiety, stress and depression; qualitative work describes people with ADHD feeling "like a burden". — [PMC12662942](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12662942/)
- Pera's "Adult ADHD-Focused Couple Therapy" is a clinician book (practice-based). — [Amazon listing](https://www.amazon.com/Adult-ADHD-Focused-Couple-Therapy-Interventions/dp/0415812100)
- Safren's protocol includes an optional partner/spouse module. — [PMC2909688](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2909688/)

### Inferences
- An app can reasonably offer partner-facing psychoeducation and shared-system tools (shared calendar, agreed handover rituals, "parent–child dynamic" awareness). These should be labelled as practice-informed, not evidence-proven.

### Gaps
- Orlov's and Tuckman's frameworks were not sourced. No RCT evidence was found.

## 6. Outcome evidence: RCTs, meta-analyses, digital CBT, combination with medication

### Takeaway
CBT for adult ADHD has moderate-to-large effects vs waitlist (SMD about 0.76–0.84) and small-to-moderate effects vs active controls (about 0.43–0.56). The evidence is mostly moderate-to-low quality with short follow-up. Effects appear stronger alongside medication. Self-guided internet CBT shows moderate-to-large effects vs psychoeducation in one Norwegian RCT, but dropout in self-guided programmes can be very high. Medication remains the only intervention with robust core-symptom effects on both rater types in the 2025 network meta-analysis.

### Cited Findings
- Young, Moghaddam & Tickle 2020 (J Atten Disord), 9 RCTs with 8 meta-analysed: CBT vs waitlist SMD 0.76 (95% CI 0.21–1.31); CBT vs active control SMD 0.43 (95% CI 0.14–0.71). — [PubMed 27554190](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27554190/)
- Cochrane (Lopez et al. 2018), 14 RCTs, n=700: CBT vs waitlist on self-reported symptoms SMD −0.84 (95% CI −1.18 to −0.50; 5 studies, n=251; moderate quality). CBT vs supportive therapy on clinician-rated symptoms SMD −0.56 (−1.01 to −0.12; 1 study, n=81; low quality). Improvements trended larger when combined with medication. Long-term data were scarce, and 13 of 14 trials were from the northern hemisphere. — [PubMed 29566425](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29566425/); [NIHR Alert](http://evidence.nihr.ac.uk/alert/cognitive-behavioural-therapy-could-benefit-adults-with-attention-deficit-hyperactivity-disorder-adhd/)
- Ostinelli et al. 2025 component NMA: stimulants and atomoxetine were the only interventions with benefits on both clinician- and self-rated core symptoms, short-term only. No medication improved quality of life. MBCT and psychoeducation were favourable, but the evidence was thin. — [PRIME/PubMed 39701638](https://www.unboundmedicine.com/medline/citation/39701638/); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/387605785)
- Kenter et al. 2023 (Internet Interventions; Norway): a self-guided 7-week internet intervention vs a psychoeducation control. Moderate-to-large between-group effects on ADHD symptoms, improved quality of life, and 58% of the intervention group showed reliable change. — [PubMed 36969389](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/36969389); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2214782923000143)
- Pettersson et al. 2017 (J Atten Disord 21(6):508–521), n=45: self-help iCBT vs iCBT plus weekly group vs waitlist. Self-guided iCBT beat waitlist on self-rated symptoms; it did not differ significantly from iCBT plus group. — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1087054714539998)
- Some self-guided internet interventions for adult ADHD have reported dropout rates of 77%. — [search summary; see Kenter feasibility study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2214782921000567)
- Further digital RCTs exist: attexis (German CBT-based DiGA) and an unguided digital intervention RCT; details not retrieved. — [attexis RCT, Psychol Med](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/psychological-medicine/article/effectiveness-of-attexis-a-digital-intervention-based-on-cognitive-behavioral-therapy-for-adults-with-adhd-a-randomized-controlled-trial/BBB55FF99ADF58005B1BD9B00AD0AF96); [PMC13475384](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13475384/)

### Inferences
- Honest app claims: "skills from CBT programmes that, delivered by therapists, show moderate benefits". Self-guided digital versions can work, but engagement is the main failure mode. Retention design (short daily practice, check-ins, optional human or peer contact) matters more than content volume.
- Comparisons against waitlist inflate effects; active-control effects (about 0.4–0.56) are the realistic benchmark.

### Gaps
- Effect sizes for attexis and the unguided-digital RCT were not retrieved.
- The Knouse 2017 and Liu 2023 effect sizes were not retrieved.

## 7. UK guidance: NICE NG87 and NHS context

### Takeaway
NICE NG87 (2018, updated 2019) makes medication first-line for adults. It offers non-pharmacological treatment when adults choose not to take medication, have difficulty adhering, or find it ineffective or not tolerated. That treatment should include, as a minimum, a structured supportive psychological intervention focused on ADHD plus regular follow-up, and it may include elements of, or a full course of, CBT. NHS assessment waits are very long (often 2+ years; up to about 8.5 years at some trusts). Right to Choose no longer reliably shortens them, so many people are undiagnosed or waiting, which is a key context for a self-help app.

### Cited Findings
- NG87: when non-pharmacological treatment is indicated for adults, offer as a minimum a structured supportive psychological intervention focused on ADHD and regular follow-up in person or by phone. Treatment may involve elements of, or a full course of, CBT. — [NICE NG87 recommendations](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng87/chapter/recommendations) (page returned 403; wording via search snippet); [NICE rationale](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng87/chapter/rationale-and-impact)
- NHS England's Independent ADHD Taskforce (final report 6 Nov 2025, chair Prof Anita Thapar): ADHD is "under-recognised, under-diagnosed and under-treated". 40% of survey respondents reported waits of 2+ years, and long waits create a two-tier system. — [Taskforce Part 2 report](https://www.england.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/PRN02228-report-of-the-independent-task-adhd-taskforce-part-2.pdf); [NHS England plain-English summary](https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/plain-english-summary-of-the-adhd-taskforce-report/)
- About 735,157 open referrals possibly for ADHD assessment in England (Dec 2025). Waits range from 12 weeks to about 8.5 years by trust. — [adhdhelper.co.uk data summary](https://adhdhelper.co.uk/data/nhs-adhd-waiting-times/) (secondary aggregator of NHS data)
- Right to Choose lets adults in England pick any NHS-funded provider, historically 6–18 months. Waits have grown since early 2025 and it is "no longer a guaranteed fast-track". — [Clinical Partners RTC page](https://www.clinical-partners.co.uk/nhs-right-to-choose-assessments-and-medication/nhs-right-to-choose-wait-times-and-updates/); [findcarecompare 2026 guide](https://findcarecompare.com/guides/adhd/nhs-adhd-waiting-times-2026/) (provider/commercial sources; treat as indicative)

### Inferences
- The NICE wording ("structured supportive psychological intervention focused on ADHD" plus "regular follow-up") is the closest UK anchor for what an app can echo. An app is not that intervention, but it can deliver structured ADHD-focused skills and regular check-ins, and it should encourage users to seek assessment and treatment.
- NHS adult ADHD psychological therapy is patchy in practice. Many people get medication only, or nothing while they wait, so a skills app fills a real gap. It must not discourage medication, given NMA evidence that medication is the strongest core-symptom treatment.

### Gaps
- The exact NG87 recommendation numbers (about 1.5.17–1.5.19) and medication first-line wording (about 1.7.x) could not be verified because of the 403. The 2018/2019 dates are UNVERIFIED (background knowledge).
- No source was found on NHS Talking Therapies' adult ADHD provision.

## 8. Self-help/digital suitability vs clinician-required

### Takeaway
Skills-practice components (planning systems, task breakdown, attention-span timers, distraction management, procrastination strategies, thought records, brief mindfulness, psychoeducation) have been delivered self-guided online with benefit. Diagnosis, medication, risk and comorbidity management, and couples therapy need a clinician. The structured therapeutic relationship (rated most helpful in COMPAS) is not replicable by an app.

### Cited Findings
- Self-guided iCBT reduced symptoms vs waitlist and did not differ significantly from iCBT plus weekly group. — [Pettersson 2017](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1087054714539998)
- The self-guided 7-week programme gave moderate-to-large effects vs psychoeducation. — [Kenter 2023](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/36969389)
- In COMPAS the group therapist was rated the most helpful component, which suggests human contact matters. — [Groß et al. 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28745107/)
- Dropout of up to 77% has been reported in self-guided internet ADHD interventions. — [search summary; Kenter feasibility](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2214782921000567)
- Repeated skill practice drives effects. — [PMC2909688](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2909688/)

### Inferences
- **Suitable for the app:** psychoeducation; a single capture list plus calendar; daily planning ritual; prioritising; task breakdown; attention-span-sized work blocks with reminders; environment/distraction audits; procrastination tools (first tiny step, if-then plans, pros/cons of delay); thought records for ADHD-typical self-critical thoughts; brief mindfulness/self-compassion; weekly review mirroring session structure.
- **Clinician-required, so signpost only:** diagnosis; medication decisions; suicidality or severe depression/anxiety; substance misuse; relationship crisis or abuse; full DBT for self-harm.
- Avoid claiming "therapy", "treatment" or "CBT programme". Prefer "skills drawn from evidence-based CBT programmes". Under UK MHRA rules, claims to treat could make the app a medical device (SaMD); that was not researched here and is flagged for verification.

### Gaps
- UK MHRA software-as-medical-device thresholds and NICE's Evidence Standards Framework for digital health were not researched in this session.
