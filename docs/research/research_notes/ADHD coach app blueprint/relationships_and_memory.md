# Relationship maintenance, social connection, and second-brain memory: an ADHD view for a private AI assistant

Research date: 2026-10-01. Scope: adults, UK context where relevant. Every claim has an inline source. Anything I could not source is listed under Gaps.

## 1. Personal CRM / relationship-maintenance apps: features, likes and dislikes

### Takeaway
Personal CRMs are split into two camps. **Automatic-capture tools** (Dex, Mesh/Clay, Covve, folk) pull interactions from email, calendar, LinkedIn and messaging, and send cadence reminders. **Deliberate manual-entry tools** (Monica, Mogul, YourPond) are privacy-first and focused on personal life. In both camps the main reason people quit is the work of entering data. The tool that wins is the one that makes the step from "we should keep in touch" to an actual message as short as possible.

### Cited Findings
- **Market shape (2026):** Monica leads in self-hosted discussion threads. Dex, Mesh (formerly Clay) and Covve lead the recommendations for dedicated apps — [getdex.com list / search summary](https://getdex.com/blog/personal-crm-list/). (Note: Dex is a vendor, so treat its comparisons with caution.)
- **Dex:** built around reminders, follow-ups and automatic tracking of past interactions taken from email, calendar and social accounts. From $12/month billed annually — [Dex vs Monica (vendor)](https://getdex.com/blog/dex-vs-monica/). Plans run $12–$40/month. It is LinkedIn-heavy and professionally focused, with no self-hosting — [Mogul shortlist, Aug 2026](https://www.mogulnetworking.com/blog/best-personal-crm-apps). One reviewer says "The reminder system is its core feature" for staying connected. Weaknesses: thin contact profiles and no mapping of relationships between contacts — [YourPond review](https://www.yourpond.io/blog/best-personal-crm-apps-2026).
- **Clay → Mesh:** Clay launched in 2018 with a well-known elegant design. It rebranded to Mesh after Automattic acquired it in June 2025 — [getdex.com list](https://getdex.com/blog/personal-crm-list/). It captures automatically from email, calendars and social networks, and offers career-change alerts and reconnection prompts. Free up to 1,000 contacts, Pro $20/month. Notes: it needs a card for the trial and has broad data access across integrations — [Mogul](https://www.mogulnetworking.com/blog/best-personal-crm-apps). AI summaries and pre-meeting talking points; the heavy automation "may feel intrusive" — [YourPond](https://www.yourpond.io/blog/best-personal-crm-apps-2026).
- **Monica:** open source and self-hostable (free) or hosted at $9/month or $90/year. Covers reminders, activity history, custom fields, relationships, full export, and personal life (friends, family, gifts, journal). No native mobile apps yet; a v3 rebuild is under way before the end of 2026 — [Mogul](https://www.mogulnetworking.com/blog/best-personal-crm-apps). Criticism: "the interface feels dated" — [YourPond](https://www.yourpond.io/blog/best-personal-crm-apps-2026).
- **Covve:** mobile-first, with business-card scanning, reminders, relationship analytics and end-to-end encryption for notes. $12–$31/month — [Mogul](https://www.mogulnetworking.com/blog/best-personal-crm-apps).
- **Nat:** timeline of interactions, contact search, basic reminders. Clean and lightweight, but no relationship mapping — [YourPond](https://www.yourpond.io/blog/best-personal-crm-apps-2026).
- **folk:** a team CRM with email, calendar and **WhatsApp sync**. $24–$48 per member per month, which is overkill for a solo user — [Mogul](https://www.mogulnetworking.com/blog/best-personal-crm-apps).
- **Mogul / YourPond:** both stress deliberate entry. YourPond adds natural-language entry ("Describe Your People"), a family tree and location maps, but has no email or LinkedIn sync — [Mogul](https://www.mogulnetworking.com/blog/best-personal-crm-apps); [YourPond](https://www.yourpond.io/blog/best-personal-crm-apps-2026).
- **Why people abandon them:** if a tool does not reduce real work ("the shortest path from 'we should keep in touch' to an actual follow-up"), users stop entering data, whatever the features — [Mogul](https://www.mogulnetworking.com/blog/best-personal-crm-apps).

### Inferences
- The core features shared across the category are: last-contact date, a target cadence per person, birthdays and key dates, free-text notes (kids' names, gift ideas, what they're dealing with), and reminder nudges. A home assistant can copy these cheaply.
- For ADHD users, manual-entry tools fail through upkeep cost, and automatic-capture tools fail on privacy and noise. The best design is probably automatic last-contact detection from the user's own data, plus a one-tap "draft me a message" step that removes the blank-page problem.
- Professional tools (Dex, Mesh, folk) are built for networking ROI. Family and friendship use needs warmer framing and no "lead scoring".

### Gaps
- I did not find independent reviews of **UpHabit, Garden or Catchup-style apps** in this pass. Whether they are still active or have shut down in 2026 is unverified.
- No systematic user-satisfaction or retention data for personal CRMs. The evidence is reviewer opinion, much of it from vendors (Dex, Mogul and YourPond each review competitors).

## 2. ADHD and relationships: out of sight out of mind, replying, friendship upkeep, mental load, rejection sensitivity

### Takeaway
ADHD adults have object permanence in the developmental sense. What they struggle with is keeping people in mind through working memory when nothing brings them to the surface. Unanswered messages pile up (often because a reply feels like it must be perfect). Forgetting gets read as not caring. Unequal household caregiving and rejection sensitivity add to this and push people towards withdrawal. Recommended fixes are external visibility, short replies, scheduled catch-up time, and telling friends openly about the difficulty.

### Cited Findings
- "Object permanence" in ADHD is a metaphor. People with ADHD do have developmental object permanence, but many struggle to keep in mind people and things that are not in front of them — [ChoosingTherapy](https://www.choosingtherapy.com/object-permanence-adhd/); [Blossom Health](https://www.joinblossomhealth.com/blog/object-permanence-adhd).
- A weak "mental scratchpad" (working memory) makes it hard to hold people in awareness without a constant external reminder. Weeks can pass without a text "not because the friendship faded, but because nothing surfaced them". The fix is visibility and low-pressure scripts for reconnecting — [Sachs Center](https://sachscenter.com/adhd-and-friendships/); [Neurodiversion](https://www.neurodiversion.org/object-permanence-adhd) (clinic blogs, not peer-reviewed).
- **ADDitude guidance for "bad texters":** timeliness matters more than how you reply. Short replies such as "It's great to hear from you! How have you been?" beat no reply. Set aside 10 minutes a day for messages (with a timer), or schedule weekly calls and texts. Tell friends: "I sometimes forget to text back… please keep texting me". Keep a "friend journal" of when you last spoke, or set email reminders — [ADDitude: Bad Texter?](https://www.additudemag.com/bad-texter-how-to-keep-in-touch-with-friends-adhd/); [ADDitude: Making Friends](https://www.additudemag.com/making-friends-strategies-for-adhd-adults/).
- **Peer-reviewed, romantic relationships:** O'Brien et al. (2025), *Journal of Marital and Family Therapy* 52(1):e70097. 355 Irish adults with ADHD (68% women, mean age 37), reflexive thematic analysis. It found four themes:
  - (1) Rejection sensitivity and emotional intensity: over-compensating, avoiding relationships out of fear, and vulnerability to toxic relationships.
  - (2) Attention and novelty-seeking: "forgetfulness creates impression I am not interested" (P396, 42M).
  - (3) **Imbalanced caregiving:** partners become de facto carers, and the person with ADHD develops learned helplessness.
  - (4) Self-understanding and diagnosis transform things: "I just accepted that I was a rotten girlfriend" (P115, 55F).
  - Source: [PMC12662942](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12662942/)
- **Mental load:** the invisible household work of planning, scheduling and noticing often falls on the non-ADHD partner. One claim says that partner carries "90%" of it — [Kindermind blog via search summary](https://kindermind.com/blog/adhd-symptoms-in-adults-relationship-impact-guide/). *Caution: the 90% figure comes from a commercial blog with no study behind it. Do not cite it as data.*
- **Friendship research:** undergraduates with higher ADHD symptoms reported more difficulty giving emotional support and handling conflict. Similar symptom levels within a pair of friends benefited the friendship — [McKee 2017, J Attention Disorders](https://doi.org/10.1177/1087054714554934). Adults with more severe ADHD symptoms want more of almost every type of support (emotional, esteem, informational, tangible) and report gaps in support — [Duede, Ray & St.Cyr Brisini 2025, J Social & Personal Relationships](https://doi.org/10.1177/02654075251332687); [PsyPost summary](https://www.psypost.org/adults-with-adhd-crave-more-relationship-support-but-often-feel-shortchanged/). Newer work on emerging adults: [Greenberg, Becker et al. 2026, J Attention Disorders](https://doi.org/10.1177/10870547261437613) (abstract not reviewed).
- **Rejection sensitivity (RSD):** not a DSM-5-TR diagnosis, and large-scale peer-reviewed measurement is still developing — [ReachLink](https://www.reachlink.com/advice/adhd/rejection-sensitive-dysphoria/). Emotional dysregulation is recognised as part of ADHD — [APA Monitor 2024](https://www.apa.org/monitor/2024/04/adhd-managing-emotion-dysregulation). Social anxiety and RSD can make friendships hard to keep "despite feeling lonely and wanting comradeship" — [Psychology Today (Saline)](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/on-your-way-with-adhd/202305/adult-adhd-and-friendship). That article cites no ADHD-specific studies.

### Inferences
- An assistant should bring people to the surface ("You haven't spoken to X in 7 weeks; last time she mentioned her mum's operation"), not just keep a list. It should also lower the bar for replying by offering 1-line drafts and "you don't need to explain the gap" framing.
- Because of RSD, prompts must not frame silence as failure or imply the other person is upset. Reach-out nudges should stress that the other person will probably welcome it (see Liu et al. in section 3).
- For partners, the most useful feature may be making household mental load visible and shared (shared calendar, birthdays, school admin), not only friend nudges.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve a CHADD primary article specifically on friendships or texting. CHADD's self-compassion article was found, but I did not read it in full.
- No quantitative study on ADHD and unanswered messages or delayed replies specifically. The evidence is clinical and anecdotal.
- No peer-reviewed quantification of how mental load is split in ADHD couples.

## 3. Evidence on social connection and wellbeing: what frequency and type of contact matters

### Takeaway
Strong social ties are linked to roughly 50% better odds of survival, an effect comparable to quitting smoking. Isolation and loneliness each carry about a 26–32% higher mortality risk. Closeness is layered (about 5/15/50/150 people), with contact frequency falling across the layers (inner circle about weekly, about 15 people about monthly, 150 about yearly). Friendships need sustained time investment (about 50 hours for a casual friend, more than 200 for a close one). Small reach-outs are valued much more than senders expect, and even brief contact with weak ties lifts daily wellbeing.

### Cited Findings
- **Holt-Lunstad, Smith & Layton (2010), PLoS Medicine 7(7):** 148 studies, 308,849 participants. Stronger social relationships gave a **50% increased likelihood of survival**. The effect is comparable to quitting smoking and larger than obesity or physical inactivity — [PLoS Medicine](https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pmed.1000316); [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2010/07/100727174909.htm).
- **Holt-Lunstad et al. (2015), Perspectives on Psychological Science 10(2):227–237:** odds ratios were social isolation 1.29, loneliness 1.26 and living alone 1.32, i.e. about 26–32% higher mortality risk — [PDF](https://local.psy.miami.edu/faculty/dmessinger/c_c/rsrcs/rdgs/emot/PerspectivesonPsychologicalScience-2015-Holt-Lunstad-227-37.pdf); [JSTOR](https://www.jstor.org/stable/44290063). The US Surgeon General advisory (2023) put loneliness and smoking at similar levels of danger — cited in [Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/on-your-way-with-adhd/202305/adult-adhd-and-friendship).
- **Dunbar's layers:** about 5 / 15 / 50 / 150 cumulative, extending to about 500 acquaintances and about 1,500 recognisable faces. The innermost layer is contacted at least weekly, the sympathy group (about 15) at least monthly, and the 150 active network at least yearly — [GoodMedicine (UK)](https://goodmedicine.org.uk/goodknowledge/social-networks-an-introduction/social-networks-dunbars-5-15-50-150-model-sympathy-group-full-active-network/); [MIT Tech Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2016/04/29/160438/your-brain-limits-you-to-just-five-bffs/); [arXiv "Calling Dunbar's Numbers"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.02400) (phone-record evidence for layers).
- **Hall (2018), Univ. of Kansas:** about 40–60 hours together to become a casual friend, 80–100 hours to become a friend, and more than 200 hours to become a close or good friend. Leisure time (hanging out, joking) counts; time working together counts much less — [KU News](https://news.ku.edu/news/article/2018/03/06/study-reveals-number-hours-it-takes-make-friend). (Published in J. Social and Personal Relationships, per KU.)
- **Liu, Rim, Min & Min (2022), J. Personality & Social Psychology:** pre-registered experiments found people consistently **underestimate how much others appreciate being reached out to**. Recipients focus on the pleasant surprise. The underestimate is larger when the contact is more surprising and when the two people are more socially distant (e.g. lapsed friends) — [APA PDF](https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/releases/psp-pspi0000402.pdf); [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2022/07/220711095206.htm).
- **Sandstrom & Dunn (2014), Personality & Social Psychology Bulletin 40(7):910–922:** on days with more interactions with weak ties (classmates, acquaintances), people felt happier and more that they belonged. Weak ties added to social and emotional wellbeing on top of strong ties. A related study found a brief friendly exchange with a barista raised positive mood — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0146167214529799); [Essex repository](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/15300/). Review of minimal social interactions as support: [Moreton et al. 2023, Social & Personality Psychology Compass](https://compass.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/spc3.12729).

### Inferences
- A sensible default cadence for an assistant follows Dunbar: weekly for the inner 5, monthly for about 15, quarterly for about 50, yearly or birthdays for 150. The user should set these, not the system.
- Liu et al. is the strongest evidence-based counter to RSD hesitation: "they'll probably appreciate this more than you think" is an empirically supported prompt, not just reassurance.
- Hall's hours suggest that for new or deepening friendships, nudges should push towards in-person or shared-activity time, not just texts.
- Weak-tie evidence supports occasional prompts for low-stakes contact (neighbours, school-gate parents, colleagues), not only close relationships.

### Gaps
- I found no robust experimental evidence on the *minimum* contact frequency needed to keep a friendship from decaying. Dunbar's frequencies are descriptive, not prescriptive.
- The 2010 and 2015 meta-analyses are correlational. Causal effects of *interventions* that increase contact on mortality are not established in these sources.
- I did not look at the newer Holt-Lunstad work (e.g. 2023–2025 updates) or UK ONS loneliness statistics.

## 4. Deriving relationship insights from message history, and the ethics of analysing other people's messages

### Takeaway
Analysing a WhatsApp export is technically routine: parse timestamps and senders, then compute message counts per person and over time, response times, who starts conversations, emoji and word frequency, and topics or sentiment, often locally in Python. In the UK, purely personal or household processing (e.g. keeping an address book, writing to friends) falls outside UK GDPR. That stops applying if there is any professional or commercial link or the data is made public. Ethically, the assistant should still minimise how much third-party content it stores. Keep derived metadata (last contact, cadence, reciprocity, short user-approved notes), not raw transcripts of other people's words.

### Cited Findings
- **Common technical metrics** from open-source WhatsApp analysers include:
  - message frequency per user and per day
  - response-time analysis between users (spotting where the sender changes and computing mean or median response time)
  - **who starts conversations**
  - emoji and word frequency, sentiment trends, media sharing, topics
  - "privacy-first, local-first" analysis of the exported .txt
  - Sources: [Rana-Faraz/whatsapp-chat-analysis](https://github.com/Rana-Faraz/whatsapp-chat-analysis); [unnyanr11/WhatsApp-Chat-Analysis](https://github.com/unnyanr11/WhatsApp-Chat-Analysis); [DevJoshi09](https://github.com/DevJoshi09/WhatsApp-Chat-Analyzer)
- More intrusive LLM-based analysers infer "mood trends, apology/gratitude frequency, personas" from chats — [RajeebLochan/Whatsapp_analysis](https://github.com/RajeebLochan/Whatsapp_analysis).
- Commercial CRMs already sync from messaging (folk has WhatsApp sync; Dex lists "messaging integrations") — [Mogul](https://www.mogulnetworking.com/blog/best-personal-crm-apps).
- **ICO, household processing:** personal data processed "in the course of a purely personal or household activity, with no connection to a professional or commercial activity, is outside the UK GDPR's scope". Examples are writing to friends and family and taking pictures for your own enjoyment — [ICO guide to exemptions](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/exemptions/a-guide-to-the-data-protection-exemptions/). Other listed examples are sharing personal photos, blogging, social media and **keeping an address book**. The data must not be connected to professional or commercial activity *or made publicly available*. Example: home CCTV used to monitor a nanny (a commercial service) is caught by the GDPR — [ICO module PDF / search summary](https://ico.org.uk/media2/migrated/4022208/module-10-exemptions-part-1.pdf); [ICO CCTV case](https://ico.org.uk/media2/migrated/4029266/ic-281826-g6m4-bundle.pdf). Strictly speaking this is outside scope rather than an "exemption", so none of the UK GDPR applies — [Irish DPC FAQ](https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/faqs/general/what-household-exemption).
- The household rule covers the *individual*. The ICO page I read does not cover service providers that process data for individuals — [ICO guide](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/exemptions/a-guide-to-the-data-protection-exemptions/).

### Inferences
- A self-hosted assistant run by the user for their own family relationships fits the household rule well, because it is like an address book plus a diary. It must stay separate from any work or commercial use. In practice, work chats and colleagues must be excluded, and the assistant must stay walled off from any business use.
- Ethics go beyond legality. People in the chats did not consent to analysis. Responsible defaults:
  1. Compute metadata (last contact, count, who started, gap length) and **drop raw message bodies** after a short summary step.
  2. Do not infer sensitive traits (mood, mental health, "personas") about third parties.
  3. Never share insights outside the household.
  4. Let the user delete any person's record.
  5. Keep processing on-prem (local LLM), not a third-party cloud.
  6. Store user-written notes about people, not verbatim quotes.
- Reciprocity metrics ("you started 80% of conversations") can feed RSD and score-keeping. Present them gently, or hide them by default and surface them only for "who have I let drift?".
- The user's own AI chat history (e.g. ChatGPT or Claude exports) mostly contains the user's own words, so the third-party concern is lower. It can still mention other people, so the same minimisation applies.

### Gaps
- I did not verify **Facebook/Messenger "Download Your Information"** export formats or WhatsApp's per-chat export limits in this pass.
- No ICO guidance found on *AI* analysis of household messages specifically, or on whether a cloud LLM provider processing them changes the analysis.
- Relevant case law (e.g. CJEU *Lindqvist*, *Ryneš* on the household exemption) was not retrieved, so it is not cited here.

## 5. Obsidian / second brain for ADHD: what helps vs "productivity procrastination"

### Takeaway
The Obsidian daily note works well for ADHD as a single low-friction capture point that links everything else. The big trap is endlessly customising the system: tags, renaming schemes, plugins, graphs. Experienced ADHD users say: keep upkeep near zero, rely on search more than structure, accept mess, and "just write the notes".

### Cited Findings
- The Obsidian Daily Note is an automatically dated note for brain dumps, tasks, wins and rants, used as "a launchpad for your day and a hub that connects everything else" — [Goodreads author blog](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/25756179-how-i-use-obsidian-daily-notes-to-actually-get-stuff-done).
- Over-engineering: people get obsessed with their Notion setups and Obsidian graphs. Elaborate setups "create a lot of work themselves". For ADHD, "anything that requires regular maintenance beyond entering today's date by hand may well be their downfall". Endless customisation is called a "Sarlacc Pit" — [Ishan Puranik Substack](https://ishanpuranik.substack.com/p/simple-adhd-productivity); [ADHD Open Space](https://adhdos.substack.com/p/adhd-pkm-showdown-notion-vs-obsidian).
- Lessons from 433 days of Obsidian by an ADHD writer:
  - (1) work with your natural organising instincts, adapting frameworks such as ACE
  - (2) the perfect system is a myth: "Your meticulously crafted system will break down within a week". Perfectionism in renaming and tagging became procrastination
  - (3) "Your notes don't need to be perfect. They just need to exist." Searchability beats structure
  - Source: [Little Yellow Different](https://www.littleyellowdifferent.com/p/3-hard-lessons-from-433-days-of-obsidian)
- The community wiki documents Dunbar-style "Nested Social Layers" as a concept note, showing Obsidian users model relationships in their vaults — [Obsidian Publish community](https://publish.obsidian.md/community/Concepts/Nested+Social+Layers).

### Inferences
- An AI assistant can take on the maintenance burden that sinks ADHD users: auto-creating daily notes, filing captures, linking people notes, and writing weekly or periodic review summaries. The user then only ever captures.
- Periodic reviews should be assistant-generated and short ("3 things from this week, 1 person to reach out to"), not a user chore.
- People notes in the vault (one per person: last contact, key dates, things they mentioned) are a natural, user-owned, plain-text personal CRM. This avoids a separate app and matches the privacy-minimisation point in section 4.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve r/ObsidianMD or r/ADHD threads directly (only blog and Substack accounts). Evidence is anecdotal; there are no studies on Obsidian/PKM and ADHD outcomes.
- No source found evaluating accountability journalling in Obsidian specifically.

## 6. Accountability: commitment devices, external accountability, self-monitoring, and avoiding shame

### Takeaway
Self-monitoring works, especially when progress is written down and reported to someone (d ≈ 0.40 on goal attainment). Commitment devices can work, but very few people take them up and they only help those who choose them. Shame and self-criticism make avoidance more likely, while self-compassion is linked to less procrastination. Accountability should therefore be external and recorded, but warm, without streaks or penalties.

### Cited Findings
- **Harkin et al. (2016), Psychological Bulletin 142(2):198–229:** 138 RCTs, N = 19,951. Interventions raised how often people monitored their progress (d = 1.98) and improved **goal attainment (d = 0.40, 95% CI 0.32–0.48)**. Effects were **larger when outcomes were reported or made public, and when the information was physically recorded** — [White Rose eprint](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/87431/).
- **Bryan, Karlan & Nelson (2010), Annual Review of Economics:** distinguishes hard and soft commitment devices. Commitment only helps those who use it, and take-up in studies has been "as low as a few percentage points" — [LSE eprint](https://eprints.lse.ac.uk/41880/); [PDF](http://houdekpetr.cz/!data/public_html/papers/economics_psychology/Bryan%20et%20al%202010.pdf). Being observed increases demand for commitment devices — [Harvard DASH paper](https://dash.harvard.edu/bitstreams/7312037e-9227-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b/download) (not read in full).
- A 2026 arXiv preprint reports that AI-assisted goal setting improved goal progress through *social accountability* — [arXiv 2603.17887](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.17887) (preprint, not read in full; treat as preliminary).
- **Shame vs self-compassion:**
  - Self-compassion, not self-criticism, is associated with less procrastination. Self-critics pursue goals to avoid failure and disapproval, and self-criticism was linked to less goal progress — [Ray Williams summary](https://raywilliams.ca/self-criticism-self-compassion-works-best/); [Mindful Institute](https://www.mindfulinstitute.org/blog/from-self-criticism-to-self-compassion-supporting-motivation-without-shame-in-adhd) (secondary sources).
  - Shame "does not improve working memory, sharpen time perception, or make starting a task easier". It adds dread that makes avoidance more likely — [ADHD Made Gentle](https://adhdmadegentle.substack.com/p/self-compassion-for-adhd-adults-why) (blog).
  - CHADD has an article on ADHD and self-compassion — [CHADD](https://chadd.org/attention-article/adhd-and-self-compassion/) (not read in full).

### Inferences
- For relationship upkeep: written logs (last-contact records) plus a gentle weekly "here's who you connected with" review match Harkin's "physically recorded + reported" moderators without public exposure. Reporting to the assistant, or optionally to a partner, may count as "reporting".
- Soft commitments (the user picks "I'll message 2 people this week") fit ADHD better than hard penalties, given low take-up of hard devices and RSD sensitivity.
- Avoid shame mechanics: no streaks, no counts of "overdue contacts" in red, no guilt framing. Celebrate any contact made, and treat lapses as information. This fits the project's no-shame principle ("constraint-based UX, no streaks or shame").

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve primary peer-reviewed studies on self-compassion and procrastination (e.g. Sirois meta-analysis); the sources here are secondary.
- No RCT found on accountability or "body doubling" interventions specifically for ADHD adults' social contact.
