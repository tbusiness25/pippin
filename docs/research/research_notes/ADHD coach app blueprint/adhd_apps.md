# Landscape of ADHD and self-care apps for adults (as of Oct 2026)

Research scope: what the leading ADHD and self-care apps do, what users love and hate, why people stop using them, what evidence exists, and which features are table stakes versus differentiators. The goal is to inform a private, self-hosted adult-ADHD companion app. Research date: 2026-10-01. About 20 search/fetch calls. Several primary sources (BusinessWire, Springer/BMC, ACM DL, medRxiv) returned 403 or required auth, so some findings rest on search snippets. Those are marked.

---

## 1. Core features, core loop and what makes each app distinctive

### Takeaway
The market splits into a few "jobs": visual time and day planning (Tiimo, Structured), CBT psychoeducation (Inflow), routine enforcement and blocking (Focus Bear), one-shot task breakdown (Goblin Tools), gamified self-care (Finch, Habitica), human coaching (Shimmer), and synchronous body doubling (Focusmate, Flow Club). Tracking apps (Bearable, Daylio) sit to one side. No single app combines all of these. That gap is the obvious opening for an integrated companion.

### Cited Findings

**Overall market framing**
- "As of June 2026, the main ADHD productivity apps split into four jobs: visual planning (Tiimo), CBT coaching (Inflow), routine enforcement and blocking (Focus Bear), and quick task decomposition (Goblin Tools)." Note that this is a vendor comparison page written by a competitor (KickMint). — [KickMint compare](https://kick-mint.com/compare/); [Super Productivity blog](https://super-productivity.com/blog/best-adhd-task-management-apps-2026/)

**Finch (self-care pet)**
- Core loop: you get a virtual pet bird and care for it by completing self-care tasks and building habits. Doing tasks gives the pet energy, sends it on "adventures" and earns rewards and cosmetics. — [Finch App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/finch-self-care-pet/id1528595748); [finchcare.com about](https://finchcare.com/about-finch)
- Suggested goals can be very small, which reviewers credit for helping with starting tasks. A neurodivergent coaching review gave it full marks for ADHD-friendliness and called it a "dopamine machine" because of rewards for every task, monthly events and new cosmetics. — [Neurodivergent Coaching review](https://www.neurodivergent-coaching.com/post/app-reviews-finch-self-care)
- Finch is deliberately forgiving and does not punish missed habits. Reviewers recommend it for people who find strict streak apps stressful. — [Just Habits Habitica review](https://just-habits.com/blog/habitica-review/)
- Finch Plus costs $69.99/yr or $9.99/mo, but the App Store lists nine separate IAP price points from $5.99 to $69.99 that vary by region and promotion. — [Finch help centre](https://help.finchcare.com/hc/en-us/articles/38755205001869-Finch-Plus-Pricing); [search summary of App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/finch-self-care-pet/id1528595748)
- 10M+ downloads on Google Play. The developer is Finch Care Public Benefit Corporation. — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.finch.finch&hl=en_US)
- Google Play's data-safety section says Finch may share personal info, financial info and other data with third parties. It encrypts data in transit and allows deletion requests. — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.finch.finch&hl=en_US) (via search snippet)

**Tiimo (visual planner)**
- Core loop: it lays your day out on a visual timeline "so time feels concrete", with routines, visual timers and icons. It was co-designed with ADHD and autism experts and has an AI co-planner, courses and family profiles. — [KickMint compare](https://kick-mint.com/compare/); [Eleven April Tiimo vs Structured](https://elevenapril.com/blog/tiimo-vs-structured)
- Awards: iPhone App of the Year 2025, Apple Design Awards finalist 2024, Best Assistive Technology 2022 (Neurodiversity Awards). 4M+ downloads. — [Daring Fireball](https://daringfireball.net/2025/12/2025_app_store_award_winners); [Wikipedia: Tiimo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiimo)
- The AI co-planner arrived in late 2025. It breaks down tasks, estimates time and builds schedules from typed or spoken input. — [Tiimo App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tiimo-productivity-ai-planner/id1480220328)
- Price is about $42–80 per year depending on platform and plan. — [KickMint compare](https://kick-mint.com/compare/)

**Structured**
- A leaner visual day planner with a larger user base than Tiimo, a Mac app and a generous free tier. The free tier includes a drag-and-drop timeline, inbox, subtasks, Pomodoro timer and sync across iPhone, iPad, Watch and Mac. Pro adds calendar connection and AI planning. — [Eleven April](https://elevenapril.com/blog/tiimo-vs-structured)

**Goblin Tools**
- "Magic ToDo" uses AI to break any task into micro-steps. It is free on the web with no account and a small one-time purchase on mobile, and processing happens in the cloud. It only does task breakdown, not scheduling, so it gets used alongside a planner. It also has tools for rewriting messages and organising thoughts. — [KickMint compare](https://kick-mint.com/compare/); [Psychelicht review](https://psychelicht.com/en/goblin-tools-review-magic-todo/)

**Inflow (CBT for ADHD)**
- Built on CBT. It delivers "science-based interactive micro-lessons, practical tools and guided support". The model is content-heavy and subscription-based, with community support. — [HIT Consultant, Mar 2026](https://hitconsultant.net/2026/03/24/cerebral-acquires-inflow-adhd-digital-therapeutic-cbt-app/); [KickMint compare](https://kick-mint.com/compare/)
- **Cerebral (US telehealth) acquired Inflow in March 2026** to "expand continuous care". Inflow is described as having been "used by hundreds of thousands of people globally". — [HIT Consultant](https://hitconsultant.net/2026/03/24/cerebral-acquires-inflow-adhd-digital-therapeutic-cbt-app/); [Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/cerebral-acquires-adhd-app-inflow-to-expand-continuous-care/)

**Focus Bear**
- Enforces morning and evening routines and blocks sites and apps. It was built by a late-diagnosed autistic/ADHD founder and uses "hard constraints". Pro is $9.99/mo and there is a free tier. — [KickMint compare](https://kick-mint.com/compare/)

**Shimmer (human ADHD coaching)**
- Weekly 1:1 video coaching, texting with your coach between sessions, a personalised plan, action-step and to-do tracking, daily community body-doubling sessions and "learning journeys". — [Shimmer site](https://www.shimmer.care/); [Shimmer App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/shimmer-care-adhd-coaching/id1570668247)
- Pricing tiers: about $140/mo (15 min weekly), $230/mo (30 min) and $345/mo (45 min). Shimmer says standard ADHD coaching costs $300–700+/mo. There is no free trial. FSA/HSA payment is accepted (US only). It claims to use "the top 4% of ADHD coaches", which is a marketing claim. — [Shimmer cost page](https://www.shimmer.care/cost); [neurolaunch](https://neurolaunch.com/shimmer-adhd-coaching/)

**Habitica**
- An RPG wrapper around habits. Completing tasks earns XP and gold. Missed "Dailies" cost HP, and if your character dies you lose a level and gear. In a party on a quest, your missed dailies damage your teammates. — [Just Habits review](https://just-habits.com/blog/habitica-review/); [Wikipedia: Habitica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habitica)

**Focusmate / Flow Club (virtual body doubling)**
- Focusmate: you book a slot and get paired 1:1 with a stranger, cameras on. You each state your goal, work for 25, 50 or 75 minutes (50 is the default), then report back. The free tier allows 3 sessions per week. — [Focusmate FAQ](https://www.focusmate.com/faq/); [flat.social review](https://flat.social/guides/focusmate-review)
- Flow Club: host-led group sessions of up to 8 people. You state your goal, work muted, then celebrate at the end. 50 minutes is the most popular length. Hosts are community volunteers. — [flat.social Flow Club review](https://flat.social/guides/flow-club-review)
- CHADD has published an explainer on Focusmate as a virtual-coworking tool for ADHD. — [CHADD](https://chadd.org/attention-article/focusmate-virtual-coworking/)

**Bearable / Daylio (mood and symptom tracking)**
- Daylio: two-tap mood logging on a 1–5 scale plus activity tags. Data stays local by default with no account. — [Bearable vs Daylio (vendor)](https://bearable.app/bearable-vs-daylio-which-one-should-you-choose/); [habitbox](https://habitbox.app/blog/best-mood-tracker-app)
- Bearable: mood on a 1–10 scale plus descriptors, symptoms, medication, sleep and "factors", then correlation insights showing which factors affect symptoms most. It has an ADHD symptom-tracker landing page and claims 900k+ users across conditions. It is a UK company, says it "will never sell" data, encrypts data at rest on GCP and claims EU GDPR compliance. — [Bearable ADHD](https://bearable.app/adhd-symptom-tracker/); [Bearable vs Daylio](https://bearable.app/bearable-vs-daylio-which-one-should-you-choose/)

**Fabulous**
- A habit and routine builder with coaching-style "journeys". Users find it engaging and visually appealing (see section 2 for billing complaints). — [habitbox Fabulous review](https://habitbox.app/blog/fabulous-app-review); [Trustpilot](https://ie.trustpilot.com/review/thefabulous.co?page=9)

**KickMint (newer entrant, for comparison)**
- Focused on starting tasks: on-device AI task breakdown (no account), a "Panic Button" for overwhelm and a "Time-Now Anchor" for time blindness. iOS only. Prices are $8.99/mo, $59.99/yr or $209.99 lifetime. Its strongest pitch is privacy. — [KickMint compare](https://kick-mint.com/compare/) (vendor's own page)

### Inferences
- Each successful app owns one job. Tiimo owns visual time, Goblin Tools owns breakdown, Finch owns warmth and forgiveness, Focusmate owns presence, and Shimmer owns human accountability. Users stitch several together, which is itself a burden: more apps, more subscriptions, more places to check.
- Inflow now belongs to a telehealth prescriber, and KickMint and Bearable both pitch privacy. This suggests privacy and independence are becoming selling points, which supports a self-hosted design.
- Shimmer's prices ($140–345/mo) show how much people value human accountability. A self-hosted AI companion cannot replace a human, but it can imitate parts of the loop: a weekly check-in, text nudges between check-ins and action-step tracking.

### Gaps
- **Not verified this session (no source fetched):** Routinery, Llama Life, Sunsama, Motion, Numo, Brili/Elevate, Todoist/Things as used by ADHD users. From general knowledge, these are: Routinery is step-by-step timed routines; Llama Life is a single-focus countdown list; Sunsama is guided daily planning and shutdown rituals; Motion is AI auto-scheduling; Numo is an ADHD community plus gamified planner; Brili is visual routines originally aimed at children. These descriptions are UNVERIFIED and should be checked before use.
- I found no independent user counts for Inflow, Focus Bear, Goblin Tools or Structured.
- I couldn't get Shimmer UK availability or GBP pricing.

## 2. What ADHD users love and hate

### Takeaway
Users love low-friction, forgiving, visual and novel experiences: Finch's tiny goals and cosmetics, Tiimo's visual timeline, Goblin Tools' instant free breakdown, and Habitica's social pressure for some people. The recurring hates are novelty wearing off, shame and punishment mechanics (streaks, HP loss), heavy setup and admin, aggressive or predatory subscriptions (Fabulous, Tiimo), buggy AI, and worries about data sharing.

### Cited Findings
- **Novelty decay:** "Every new planner is novel for about a week… when the newness wears off, the effort of using it stays, and one missed day can tip the whole thing into the drawer." — [Affine blog on planner abandonment](https://affine.pro/blog/planner-abandonment-adhd-solutions); also [ADHD Without the Guilt substack, "Why every planner dies after 2 weeks"](https://adhdwithouttheguilt.substack.com/p/why-every-planner-dies-after-2-weeks) (these are blog and opinion sources, not studies)
- Systems that last are described as visible and glanceable, able to survive missed days ("restartable"), low on admin, and free of shame. — [Affine blog](https://affine.pro/blog/planner-abandonment-adhd-solutions) (opinion)
- **Streak and punishment shame:** Habitica's HP-loss mechanic "turns missed habits into anxiety triggers, especially once you're invested in a party". The reviewer links this to the abstinence violation effect, where a single slip leads to giving up entirely. — [Just Habits](https://just-habits.com/blog/habitica-review/); [Habitdoom "Streak anxiety"](https://habitdoom.com/blog/streak-anxiety-habit-trackers)
- **But some love it:** one neurodivergent reviewer called Habitica "basically ADHD medication" because of the social pressure and instant rewards. — [Just Habits](https://just-habits.com/blog/habitica-review/)
- **Finch, loved:** forgiving, very small goals, rewards for every task, constant new cosmetics through monthly events. — [Neurodivergent Coaching](https://www.neurodivergent-coaching.com/post/app-reviews-finch-self-care)
- **Tiimo, hated:** its AI assistant is called buggy and frustrating and misreads commands and calendars. Other complaints are steep pricing, timer bugs, a limited free tier and notifications too brief ("wanting them to last longer than the typical 'ding'"). Some users call the year-long free trial with no cancel reminder "predatory" given it is marketed to ADHD and autistic people. — [justuseapp Tiimo reviews (aggregated App Store reviews)](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1480220328/tiimo-ai-planner-to-do/reviews); [anygen review](https://www.anygen.io/showcase/tiimo-review/index.html)
- **Fabulous, hated:** trouble cancelling trials, charges after cancelling, extra packages auto-subscribed, a confusing sequence of promotional prices, and poor support. Users still praise how engaging the design is. — [Trustpilot](https://ie.trustpilot.com/review/thefabulous.co?page=9); [habitbox](https://habitbox.app/blog/fabulous-app-review); [pissedconsumer](https://www.pissedconsumer.com/fabulous/RT-F.html)
- **Focusmate, mixed:** a body-doubling researcher found Focusmate effective for some kinds of work but said its "high social pressure and accountability can be stressful or overwhelming". — [search summary citing ACM TACCESS 2024 paper](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3689648) (full text was 403)
- **Privacy:** Finch's Play data-safety section allows sharing with third parties. Daylio is local by default. Bearable leads its pitch with "never sell". KickMint markets on-device AI. — sources as in section 1

### Inferences
- Shame-free design and the ability to restart after a missed day ("you're back, here's today") matter more than any single feature. Avoid streak counters, HP loss and "you missed X days" copy. This is consistent with the project's no-shame principle (constraint-based UX, no streaks, no shame).
- Subscription pain is very visible in reviews. A self-hosted app avoids it completely, which is an advantage worth keeping in mind.
- Notifications need to be adjustable both ways. Some ADHD users want *longer and more persistent* alerts, not fewer.

### Gaps
- I couldn't fetch Reddit (r/ADHD, r/adhdwomen) threads directly. The "love/hate" evidence above comes from review aggregators, blogs and app-store summaries rather than primary Reddit posts.
- I found no structured sentiment analysis of ADHD app reviews.

## 3. Retention: drop-off and patterns that sustain use

### Takeaway
Unguided mental-health apps lose more than 80% of users within 10 days. Median 30-day retention is about 3.3%. Trackers and peer-support apps keep people somewhat better. Even an evidence-based ADHD app (Inflow) lost about half its users in 7 weeks, with very low median content completion. Design ideas that may sustain ADHD use: social presence and accountability, forgiving and restartable systems, tiny steps, visual time, low-friction capture, and ongoing variety. Apart from the general app data, the evidence for these is mostly observational or anecdotal.

### Cited Findings
- Baumel et al. 2019 (JMIR, panel data on popular unguided mental-health apps): median 15-day retention 3.9% (IQR 10.3%), median **30-day retention 3.3%** (IQR 6.2%). App opens fall by more than 80% between day 1 and day 10, then by about 20% more between day 15 and day 30. — [JMIR 2019;21(9):e14567](https://www.jmir.org/2019/9/e14567/)
- By technique, day-30 retention was higher for peer support (median 8.9%, n=2 apps), trackers (median 6.1%, IQR 20.4%) and mindfulness (median 4.7%) than for breathing apps (median 0.0%). — [JMIR 2019](https://www.jmir.org/2019/9/e14567/)
- Inflow feasibility study (Knouse et al. 2022, PLOS Digital Health): 52.7% of enrolled users (108/205) were retained at 7 weeks, and 54% dropped out before week 7. Median engagement was 27 sessions over 7 weeks (about 3.9/week), a median session length of 3.4 minutes, and a **median of just 1 module and 1 challenge completed**. — [PMC9931323](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9931323/)
- In the Inflow RCT, higher engagement with exercises was associated with greater self-reported improvement in attention. This is correlational. — [HIT Consultant](https://hitconsultant.net/2026/03/24/cerebral-acquires-inflow-adhd-digital-therapeutic-cbt-app/)
- In a survey (Eagle et al.), 186 of 220 participants said they were more likely or somewhat more likely to finish a task with another person present. — [search summary; Eagle et al. / ACM TACCESS 2024](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3689648) (not verified in full text)
- People with ADHD "consistently described discovering [body doubling] on their own before they ever had a name for it". — [ACM TACCESS 2024](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3689648) (via search summary)
- Advice on sustaining use (opinion): stay visible, be restartable, automate admin, be shame-free, offer variety and customisation. — [Affine blog](https://affine.pro/blog/planner-abandonment-adhd-solutions)

### Inferences
- The Inflow pattern of frequent, short sessions (about 3.4 minutes, about 4 times a week) alongside almost no course completion suggests that ADHD users engage with *quick tools and check-ins*, not courses. A companion should put the lightweight daily loop first and slip micro-learning into it, rather than gate it behind modules.
- The finding that peer support and trackers keep more users fits with body doubling and self-monitoring as the stickiest mechanisms. For a private single-household app, "presence" could be an AI or ambient body double, or real family presence. That is untested.
- Plan for a "lapse and return" path from the start: most users will lapse, so a good re-entry experience is worth more than trying to stop every drop-off.

### Gaps
- I found no published retention curves for Finch, Tiimo, Habitica or Focusmate specifically. Their company metrics are not public.
- I found no controlled evidence comparing gamified and forgiving designs in ADHD adults.

## 4. Evidence for app-based interventions in adult ADHD

### Takeaway
Meta-analyses show digital interventions reduce ADHD symptoms overall. The pooled samples mix children and adults and many intervention types (games, cognitive training, VR, apps), and the quality of evidence is generally low to moderate. Adult-specific app evidence is thin. Inflow is the best-known: an uncontrolled feasibility study with large within-group effects and heavy dropout, run by the company's own team, and an 8-week RCT reported positively in press materials. Body doubling has almost no controlled evidence. I found no RCTs of AI task-breakdown tools. EndeavorRx (FDA-cleared video game) is for children aged 8–17 and is not relevant to adults.

### Cited Findings
- A 2024 meta-analysis of **25 RCTs (n=1,780)** found digital interventions significantly reduced overall ADHD symptoms, inattention and hyperactivity/impulsivity compared with controls. The interventions included video games, computerised cognitive training, VR and apps. The sample mixed ages. — [PubMed 39191306](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39191306/)
- A 2025 meta-analysis of digital mental-health interventions for ADHD found telehealth, mHealth and combined approaches had comparable efficacy. The effect size was in the range seen in face-to-face CBT meta-analyses for adult ADHD. — [ScienceDirect S2451958825001186](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2451958825001186) (via search summary)
- A 2025 systematic review of reviews (BMC Psychiatry) on the effectiveness and safety of digital ADHD interventions reported adverse effects including physical discomfort, emotional reactions and behavioural issues such as gaming addiction. — [BMC Psychiatry 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12888-025-06825-0) (full text blocked; finding from search summary)
- **Inflow feasibility (Knouse et al. 2022, PLOS Digital Health):** open trial over 7 weeks with no control group. Among 93 completers: inattention d = −1.0, hyperactive-impulsive d = −0.41, total d = −0.71 (BAARS), functional impairment (BFIS) d = −0.46, all p<.001. **Conflicts of interest:** two authors are Inflow co-founders with ownership stakes, and the lead author is an unpaid Inflow consultant. Limitations: 54% dropout, mostly White and educated sample, self-report only, completer bias. — [PMC9931323](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9931323/)
- **Inflow RCT:** an 8-week RCT reportedly showed significant improvements in ADHD symptoms and ADHD-related quality of life. — [HIT Consultant](https://hitconsultant.net/2026/03/24/cerebral-acquires-inflow-adhd-digital-therapeutic-cbt-app/); [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260324103905/en/Cerebral-Acquires-Leading-ADHD-Behavioral-App-Inflow-Expanding-Access-to-Continuous-Care) (press release, 403 on fetch). I could not find the journal citation, control condition or effect sizes, so treat this as a marketing claim until verified.
- **attexis:** a 2025 medRxiv preprint reports an RCT of attexis, a digital intervention for adult ADHD (a German DiGA-type product). The preprint was not peer-reviewed at the time and the full text returned 403. — [medRxiv 2025.08.27.25334574](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.08.27.25334574v1.full.pdf)
- A self-guided internet-delivered intervention for adult ADHD has a published RCT protocol. — [PMC8632851](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8632851/)
- **Body doubling:** "No peer-reviewed controlled trials have examined its impact on adults with ADHD." Evidence consists of qualitative work (ACM TACCESS 2024), surveys, and a small VR lab study (n=12) where participants with a human or AI body double finished tasks faster and reported better sustained attention. — [arXiv 2509.12153 VR body doubling](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.12153); [PopSci](https://www.popsci.com/health/body-doubling-focus-benefits/); [ACM TACCESS](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3689648)
- A 2026 scoping review of assistive technologies for adults with ADHD exists as an arXiv preprint. — [arXiv 2601.21791](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.21791) (not read in full)

### Inferences
- The strongest evidence base for adults is CBT-derived content: planning, organisation, managing distractibility and cognitive restructuring. Digital delivery appears roughly comparable to face-to-face in pooled analyses. A companion that builds in CBT skills such as implementation intentions, task breakdown and time estimation rests on firmer ground than gamification.
- Most claims for productivity apps (Tiimo, Goblin Tools, Focus Bear, Structured, Finch) are untested. Their "for ADHD" positioning is marketing, not evidence.
- AI body doubling showed a promising signal in one tiny VR study. That is a hypothesis worth testing, not a proven feature.

### Gaps
- Full citation, control arm and effect sizes for the Inflow RCT.
- Results of the attexis RCT.
- I found no RCTs of AI task-breakdown tools (Goblin Tools, Tiimo co-planner) on ADHD outcomes.
- I found no RCTs of Finch, Habitica or Tiimo.
- I did not search UK NICE/NHS digital-health guidance on ADHD apps (for example, NICE early value assessment).

## 5. Table stakes vs genuine differentiators

### Takeaway
Table stakes in 2026: quick task capture, reminders, visual timeline or timer, AI task breakdown (now standard in Tiimo, Structured Pro, Goblin Tools and KickMint), routine templates, cross-device sync, and a forgiving tone. Real differentiators: human or social accountability (Shimmer, Focusmate), a warm companion that responds to your effort (Finch), evidence-based CBT content (Inflow), hard constraints and blocking (Focus Bear), personal correlation insights (Bearable), and, increasingly, privacy and on-device processing.

### Cited Findings
- AI breakdown is now everywhere: Tiimo's AI co-planner (late 2025), Structured Pro's AI planning, Goblin Tools' Magic ToDo, and KickMint's on-device decomposition. — [Tiimo App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tiimo-productivity-ai-planner/id1480220328); [Eleven April](https://elevenapril.com/blog/tiimo-vs-structured); [KickMint compare](https://kick-mint.com/compare/)
- Visual timelines are offered by both Tiimo and Structured, and Structured gives one away free. — [Eleven April](https://elevenapril.com/blog/tiimo-vs-structured)
- Human accountability costs a lot ($140–345/mo for Shimmer), and Shimmer adds community body doubling. — [Shimmer cost](https://www.shimmer.care/cost)
- Privacy as a selling point: Daylio local-first, Bearable "never sell", KickMint "AI fully on the iPhone". — sources in section 1
- Correlation insights (which factors drive symptoms) are Bearable's distinguishing feature compared with Daylio. — [Bearable vs Daylio](https://bearable.app/bearable-vs-daylio-which-one-should-you-choose/)

### Inferences
- For a private, self-hosted companion, the defensible combination is: (a) shame-free, restartable daily loop; (b) AI task breakdown and time estimation via a local LLM, which is expected anyway; (c) a sense of presence and accountability (an AI body-double or check-in persona, plus optional family visibility); (d) Bearable-style personal correlation across mood, sleep, medication and task data, which self-hosting makes possible without privacy cost; (e) CBT-derived micro-skills built into the loop rather than offered as courses.
- Leaving out subscriptions, third-party data sharing and streak or punishment mechanics is in itself a differentiator, given how prominent those complaints are.

### Gaps
- No user-research data ranks features by importance for ADHD adults. This is inferred from reviews and marketing.
- I did not assess Motion, Sunsama, Todoist or Things on ADHD-specific features (see section 1 gaps).
