# What Professional ADHD Coaches Do with Adult Clients

## Standards and frameworks (ICF, PAAC, ADDCA, Edge, ACO); how ADHD coaching differs from life coaching and therapy

### Takeaway
ADHD coaching combines generic coaching competencies (ICF's 8) with an ADHD-specific layer. PAAC's "Five Essentials" describe that layer best: ADHD Lens, Safety (no shame), Collaboration, Wonder and Action. It is a wellness and skills model about the "what, when and how" of daily life. It is not therapy. It does not diagnose, treat comorbidities or process trauma, and it is meant to complement medication and CBT.

### Cited Findings
- **ICF (generic coaching)** has 8 core competencies: Demonstrates Ethical Practice, Embodies a Coaching Mindset, Establishes and Maintains Agreements, Cultivates Trust and Safety, Maintains Presence, Listens Actively, Evokes Awareness and Facilitates Client Growth. They sit in 4 domains (Foundation, Co-Creating the Relationship, Communicating Effectively, Cultivating Learning and Growth). The 2025 update kept the 8 competencies, added 5 sub-competencies and revised 11 others. It was based on a job analysis of about 3,000 coaches. — [Tandem Coach summary of ICF 2025](https://tandemcoach.co/icf-core-competencies-2025-update/); [ICF comparison](https://coachingfederation.org/resource/icf-core-competencies-comparison/) (secondary summaries; the ICF primary page was not fetched)
- **PAAC Five Essentials** (fetched verbatim):
  1. **ADHD Lens**: "recognizing how ADHD shows up in the moment and meeting the client where they are". Markers include Interest, Pause, Structure, Sensitivity, Impulsivity, Executive Functions, Working Memory and Assistive Technology.
  2. **Safety**: "think clearly, feel seen, and take honest risks — without shame". Markers include Permission, Compassion, Acceptance and Celebration.
  3. **Collaboration**: "the client's choices, language, and priorities shape the direction". Markers include Client Choice, Agenda, Co-creation and Truth Telling.
  4. **Wonder**: "space for meaning, identity, and what the client most wants — not just what they are struggling with". Markers include Limiting Beliefs, Assumptions and Brainstorming.
  5. **Action**: "translate awareness into movement, and movement into something the client can actually sustain". Markers include Priorities, Structure, Accountability, Experiments, Winning Strategies and Follow Through.

  — [PAAC: The 5 Essentials](https://paaccoaches.org/the-5-essentials/)
- PAAC's framework has four pillars. Competencies are defined by the Essentials, observed through Markers, assessed through recorded real sessions (Performance) and recognised by tiered credentials: CACP (Certified ADHD Coach Practitioner), PCAC (Professional Certified ADHD Coach) and MCAC (Master Certified ADHD Coach). — [PAAC Standards](https://paaccoaches.org/standards/)
- **ADDCA's "QuestVersation" model** uses six skills: receiving, witnessing, questioning, filtering, activating and reinventing. ADDCA says it integrates both the ICF (life) and PAAC (ADHD) competencies. — [ADDCA Basic Module Details](https://addca.com/adhd-coach-training/Module-Details-Basic/)
- **ADHD Coaches Organization (ACO)** is a membership and advocacy body, not a credentialing body. Members follow their own certifying body's ethics and must represent their credentials honestly. — [ACO Standards and Expectations](https://www.adhdcoaches.org/standards-and-expectations)
- **CHADD (help4adhd) on coaching versus CBT:**
  - Most coaching is based on CBT principles, and both share many elements: goal setting, prioritisation, motivation, organisation, planning and scheduling, problem solving, stress management, impulse control, confidence, communication, memory and homework.
  - Coaching, however, is "a wellness model and not designed to help a client heal or deal with psychological barriers". It focuses on daily living such as finances, home, nutrition, exercise and sleep. Coaches "focus on what, when and how but rarely why".
  - CBT covers emotional regulation and treats comorbid anxiety, depression and substance use.
  - Coaching is also not tutoring. It "presumes the individual being coached is the expert". Strategies are offered as suggestions and the client decides which to try.

  — [CHADD/help4adhd: ADHD Coaching for Adults (PDF)](https://chadd.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/coaching-adults.pdf)
- CHADD's evidence-based coaching article says "psychiatrists, therapists, and coaches have non-overlapping areas of expertise" and work best as a coordinated three-part approach. It sees clients as "creative, resourceful, and whole". — [CHADD: Evidence-Based Coaching for Adults with ADHD](https://chadd.org/attention-article/evidence-based-coaching-for-adults-with-adhd/)
- ADDitude describes coaching as "an ongoing collaborative partnership created to facilitate greater self-awareness, self-regulation, and self-initiative". It notes ADHD coaching "requires more time" than general life coaching because of executive-function (EF) barriers. — [ADDitude: CBT vs ADHD coaching](https://www.additudemag.com/adhd-therapy-cbt-vs-coaching/)
- **Regulation:**
  - The Australian ADHD Clinical Practice Guideline notes coaches are not regulated by AHPRA and not all hold ICF membership, so it advises "consumer caution".
  - It also notes that coaching's environmental and behavioural strategies overlap with OT and psychology.

  — [AADPA Guideline: ADHD Coaching](https://adhdguideline.aadpa.com.au/non-pharmacological/adhd-coaching/)
- A 2026 Medscape piece ("The Boom in ADHD Coaching Has Few Rules") and an ADDitude study report both say coaching is growing while training and licensure stay uneven. — [Medscape 2026](https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/boom-adhd-coaching-has-few-rules-2026a10005iw) (paywalled; only the title and snippet were seen); [ADDitude](https://www.additudemag.com/adhd-coaching-popular-mental-health/)

### Inferences
- PAAC's Five Essentials map neatly onto product requirements for an AI coach:
  - **ADHD Lens**: psychoeducation that normalises and names patterns as they happen.
  - **Safety**: a shame-free tone and no streak or shame mechanics. This fits the project's no-shame principle.
  - **Collaboration**: the client sets the agenda and the AI offers suggestions rather than prescriptions.
  - **Wonder**: values, identity and possibility questions.
  - **Action**: every conversation ends in a concrete, sustainable next step.
- The coaching stance is "ask, then suggest". An AI should default to Socratic questions, but still offer concrete ADHD strategies when asked or when the client is stuck. ADHD coaching is more directive and psychoeducational than pure ICF coaching.

### Gaps
- The full ICF 2025 sub-competency text came only from secondary summaries.
- Edge Foundation's own coaching protocol (cadence, check-in rules) was not available on the page fetched.
- I did not find the UK-specific bodies' (ADHD UK, ADDISS) positions on coaching standards.

## Barkley's EF model, "point of performance", and externalising information, time and motivation

### Takeaway
Barkley frames ADHD as a disorder of self-regulation. Internally held information (plans, rules, a sense of time) and self-generated motivation are too weak to steer behaviour, so help has to be delivered at the *point of performance*: the time and place where the person fails to use what they already know. In practice that means:
- making information physical
- shortening time gaps by doing a step a day with immediate feedback
- creating external, often artificial, motivation and sustaining it long term

His own claim is that weekly counselling alone, with nothing inserted into daily life, "is unlikely to succeed".

### Cited Findings
- "The point of performance is that place and time in the natural setting of the person's life where they are failing to use what they know… Once per week counseling without efforts to insert accommodations at key points of performance in natural settings is unlikely to succeed." — [Barkley, ADHD, EF and Self-Regulation fact sheet (PDF)](https://www.russellbarkley.org/factsheets/ADHD_EF_and_SR.pdf)
- **Externalising information:** "To 'externalize' information is to make it physical outside of the individual." The solution "is not to nag… to simply try harder or to remember". Instead you "take charge of that immediate context and fill it with forms of physical cues". Distractors should be minimised and replaced with task-related cues that are "just as salient and appealing". Rules can be posted as signs, stated aloud before and during work, or played as recorded reminders through an earphone. — same source
- **Externalising time:** "time and the future are the enemies of people with EF difficulties". The temporal gaps in a task should be reduced. "Rather than tell them that a project must be done over the next month, assist them with doing a step a day toward that eventual goal… in small daily work periods with immediate feedback and incentives." — same source
- **Externalising motivation:** "Internal sources of motivation must be augmented with more powerful external forms." "Artificial means of creating external sources of motivation must be arranged at the point of performance." These "prosthetic forms of motivation must be sustained for long periods". Complaining about laziness, or pulling back to let natural consequences "teach a lesson", is "a recipe for disaster". — same source
- **Replenishing self-regulation resources:** Barkley cites self-regulation "resource pool" research, and the fact sheet lists factors that may help replenish it:
  - routine exercise
  - 10-minute breaks during demanding tasks
  - at least 3 minutes of relaxation or meditation after them
  - visualising the reward or outcome during the task
  - periodic small rewards
  - self-efficacy affirmations before and during tasks
  - positive emotion
  - glucose drinks

  Note: the ego-depletion literature behind this has since faced replication problems. That is my caveat, not Barkley's. — same source
- Barkley sees medication as the only treatment shown to normalise EF, and only while it is active. He cites behavioural improvement in 75–92% and normalisation in about 50–60%. — same source
- Popular syntheses restate the principle that "a plan you are holding in your head… is a weak source of control for an ADHD brain" and that visible cues at the moment of action are stronger. — [Science Works Health: External systems for ADHD at work](https://www.scienceworkshealth.com/post/external-systems-for-adhd-at-work)

### Inferences
- This is the strongest design argument for an AI coach. A weekly-session model is not enough, and the value lies in proactive nudges at the moment of action. Examples:
  - time-of-task push notifications
  - "starting now?" prompts
  - visible timers
  - check-ins at the start and end of a work block
  - daily micro-steps toward long projects
  - immediate small rewards or acknowledgement
- The AI should never "let natural consequences teach", and it should sustain external scaffolds indefinitely rather than fade them by default.
- Turning big deadlines into a step a day with immediate feedback maps directly onto a "today's one step" feature for multi-week goals.

### Gaps
- I did not find direct published evidence of how coaches apply "point of performance" operationally (for example, exact nudge frequency). It is inferred from coaching descriptions of between-session check-ins (see the session-structure question).

## Concrete techniques coaches use

### Takeaway
The core repertoire is:
- breaking tasks into tiny behavioural steps
- if-then plans that cover barriers
- external supports (timers, checklists, visible cues)
- taking action during the session itself
- "book-end" check-ins before and after hard tasks
- weekly goal review and planning
- interest-based motivation (Dodson's Interest, Challenge/Competition, Novelty, Urgency)
- body doubling
- environment design
- shame reduction

Evidence ranges from good (implementation intentions in general and in children with ADHD) to anecdotal (body doubling).

### Cited Findings
- **CHADD's core components of evidence-based adult ADHD coaching:**
  1. Small steps in behavioural language, with tasks broken down.
  2. Realistic expectations backed by external supports such as timers and checklists.
  3. Immediate action-taking in session (a "hands-on approach").
  4. Between-session accountability.
  5. Moving from overwhelm to structured planning.

  It also describes "book-end" check-ins before and after challenging actions, and draws on self-regulation science, implementation science and ADHD-focused CBT. — [CHADD: Evidence-Based Coaching for Adults with ADHD](https://chadd.org/attention-article/evidence-based-coaching-for-adults-with-adhd/)
- **Typical coaching questions (CHADD):**
  - "What changes do you want to make in your daily life?"
  - "What small steps can you take today in the direction of your goals?"
  - "How can you motivate yourself to take action towards this goal?"
  - "When must this action be completed?"
  - "What steps have you taken already, and when will you take the remaining steps?"
  - "How will you evaluate the impact of your plan?"

  Coaches help clients "translate abstract goals into concrete actions" and "find ways to use concrete and abstract rewards effectively", and may send reminders or suggest time-management methods. — [CHADD/help4adhd PDF](https://chadd.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/coaching-adults.pdf)
- **Implementation intentions:** "If I encounter Situation X then I will do Y". Meeting the situation triggers the planned behaviour automatically. — [Springer/START summary](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12888-023-05181-1)
- **Gawrilow & Gollwitzer (2008, Cognitive Therapy and Research 32:261–280):** in children with ADHD, if-then plans raised Go/NoGo inhibition to the level of children without ADHD. If-then plans combined with stimulant medication gave the best inhibition. — [Univ. Konstanz](https://www.socmot.uni-konstanz.de/publications/implementation-intentions-facilitate-response-inhibition-adhd-children); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10608-007-9150-1)
- A later paper found that mental contrasting with implementation intentions (MCII, also known as WOOP) improved goal pursuit in schoolchildren at risk for ADHD. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/257623701_Mental_contrasting_with_implementation_intentions_enhances_self-regulation_of_goal_pursuit_in_schoolchildren_at_risk_for_ADHD)
- A meta-analysis (Toli et al., 2016, *British Journal of Clinical Psychology*) examined implementation intentions in clinical and analogue mental-health samples. I saw only the title and did not extract its effect size. — [Wiley](https://bpspsychub.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/bjc.12086)
- **Adult ADHD CBT built on implementation:** "Turning Intentions into Actions" CBT for adult ADHD breaks goals into feasible steps, decides where each step happens, and writes if-then plans for the step and for its barriers. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/283239518_Turning_Intentions_Into_Actions_CBT_for_Adult_ADHD_Focused_on_Implementation)
- **CBT-informed app RCT:** adults with ADHD (n=154, aged 18–55) using a CBT-informed app reported improved inattentive and hyperactive-impulsive symptoms and quality of life versus controls. This is from the search summary; I did not verify the primary paper or name the app. — [search result, START/Springer cluster](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12888-023-05181-1)
- **Interest-based nervous system (Dr William Dodson):**
  - ADHD attention engages with Interest, Challenge/Competition, Novelty or Urgency (ICNU), not mere importance. INCUP is a popular variant that adds Passion.
  - The suggested practice is a motivation log noting when you felt engaged, what you were doing and the context, so patterns emerge over a few weeks.
  - This is a clinical heuristic, not a validated construct.

  — [Neurodivergent Insights](https://neurodivergentinsights.com/working-with-your-interest-based-nervous-system/); [Inflow on INCUP](https://www.getinflow.io/post/adhd-motivation-incup)
- **Body doubling:** no peer-reviewed controlled trials show effectiveness in adults with ADHD.
  - An EEG study found no statistically significant difference from working alone, though it suggests possible benefit for unmedicated people.
  - A VR study (n=12) found faster completion and better perceived attention with human or AI body doubles.
  - A 2024 robot-companion study found 91% of students chose to keep using the robot after a week.

  — [Medical News Today](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/body-doubling-adhd); [ACM EEG study](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3663547.3759743); [arXiv VR study](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.12153)
- ADDA describes the body double as a long-standing community technique. — [ADDA: The ADHD Body Double](https://add.org/the-body-double/)
- **Shame and self-compassion:**
  - Adults with ADHD report high perceived criticism, which contributes to self-reproach, shame and guilt. Self-compassion is being studied as a basis for recovery-oriented interventions. — [PMC12033728](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12033728/)
  - Compassion-focused approaches are proposed for adults with high shame. — [Psychology Today: Self-Compassion and Adult ADHD](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/rethinking-adult-adhd/202209/self-compassion-and-adult-adhd)
  - Some search snippets claimed specific statistics ("shame most predictive of self-concept impairment", "emotional dysregulation in over 70%"). They came from unverified aggregators and are left out as unreliable.
- **Barkley-derived techniques** (see the previous question): posted rules, rules said aloud, recorded reminders, distractor removal, a step a day, immediate rewards, 10-minute breaks, exercise and visualising the outcome. — [Barkley fact sheet](https://www.russellbarkley.org/factsheets/ADHD_EF_and_SR.pdf)
- **Coaching topics list (CHADD; shared with CBT):** goal setting, prioritisation, motivation, organisation, planning and scheduling, problem solving, stress management, impulse control, confidence and self-esteem, relationships and communication, memory, homework, plus daily-living habits (finances, home, nutrition, exercise, sleep). — [CHADD/help4adhd PDF](https://chadd.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/coaching-adults.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Conversation-flow primitives for an AI coach:**
  1. **Goal to step.** Turn a vague goal into one next physical action of 2–10 minutes.
  2. **If-then plan.** "When [cue/time/place], I will [step]". Then a barrier plan: "If [obstacle], then I will [response]".
  3. **WOOP/MCII** for bigger goals: Wish, Outcome, Obstacle, Plan.
  4. **Book-end check-ins.** "Tell me when you start" and "how did it go?", with a timer.
  5. **Body-double mode.** The AI stays present through a timed block. This is low evidence but low risk.
  6. **Motivation lens.** Ask which of ICNU can be added to a boring task (gamify it, add a deadline, novelty, a partner).
  7. **Shame-free debrief.** Reframe a missed task as data: "what got in the way? what would you change?"
- Time-blindness features would follow from Barkley: visual countdowns, practising estimates (predict how long, then compare with actual) and shrinking gaps by making the future present.

### Gaps
- I did not find peer-reviewed evidence specifically for time-estimation practice, visual timers or specific prioritisation methods (Eisenhower, 1-3-5) in adult ADHD coaching. These are widely recommended but unvalidated in this population as far as I found.
- Values work and environment design are mentioned by PAAC (Wonder/Meaning) and Barkley (context), but I found no distinct protocols for them.

## Session structure, cadence, intake and progress tracking

### Takeaway
The standard format has five parts:
- **Intake:** 1–2 hours, covering whole-life satisfaction, history, medication, sleep, strengths and three long-term goals, with screening for mental-health risk.
- **Regular sessions:** usually weekly, 30–60 minutes. Review last week's goals, reflect on what helped or hindered, then plan next week's steps.
- **Between sessions:** brief check-ins by text, email or phone in whatever format the client prefers.
- **Contract:** a fixed length, commonly 8–12 sessions in research protocols.
- **Close:** an evaluation session.

### Cited Findings
- "The first coaching session is typically an in-depth, 1–2 hour meeting to allow clients to reflect on their satisfaction in all areas of life and to develop clear, long-term goals."
- "Regular coaching sessions may last 30 to 60 minutes and are used to report progress on the previous week's goals, reflect on factors enhancing and inhibiting progress and develop a step-by-step plan for… the next week's goals."
- An end-of-contract evaluation session decides whether to continue, adjust or stop.
- Sessions can be held in person, online, by phone, email or text, depending on the client's preference.
- "Regular meetings and check-ins are an essential part of the coaching process."

— [CHADD/help4adhd PDF](https://chadd.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/coaching-adults.pdf)
- **Readiness for coaching:** the client must admit there is a problem, want to change, be willing to spend time building strategies and be willing to stick to them. — same source
- **APA intake protocol** (from Prevatt-style college coaching; *ADHD Coaching* book supplement):
  - **Pre-coaching forms:** ASICS; BDEFS (Barkley Deficits in Executive Functioning Scale), listing domains rated 3 ("very often"); a symptom checklist covering depression and anxiety; a Coaching Topics Survey (list items rated 1, "definitely need to work on"); a current ADHD symptoms scale; OQ-45; and the Rosenberg Self-Esteem Scale.
  - **OQ-45 items to flag:** "I feel worthless", "I feel hopeless about the future", intrusive disturbing thoughts, anger "enough… to do something I might regret", and "thoughts or feelings of ending my life".
  - **Intake questions:**
    - Why are you seeking coaching?
    - Have you had coaching before? What worked, and what would you do differently?
    - Are you in counselling now?
    - What medication are you on (with dosage), when did you start, and are there problems (sleep, effectiveness, eating, forgetting doses)?
    - How is your sleep, and how many hours do you get? How is your eating? Do you eat breakfast, and what?
    - Work strengths and difficulties.
    - Relationships.
    - What are the biggest obstacles to your goals?
    - What coping strategies do you use now?
    - What are your strengths?
    - What do you do for fun?
    - What social support do you have?
    - How do you see yourself?
    - Any current mental-health diagnosis?
    - Any family history of ADHD, learning disabilities or mental-health conditions?
  - **Outputs of intake:** three long-term goals, a fixed weekly session day and time, and a preference for text, email or phone reminders.

  — [APA: The Intake Interview (PDF)](https://www.apa.org/pubs/books/supplemental/ADHD-Coaching/The_Intake_Interview.pdf)
- **ACE-MI manual (Ahmann & Saviet, 2024, *International Journal of Evidence Based Coaching and Mentoring* 22(1):177–198):** 12 individual sessions in three phases.
  - **Initial:** history and functioning, strengths and values, envisioning goals.
  - **Ongoing:** review progress, explore challenges, brainstorm strategies, commit to actions.
  - **Closing:** summarise growth, consolidate learning, plan for the future.
  - Elements include an "ADHD lens", "promoting growth and hope", psychoeducation, affirming strengths, self-advocacy, EF assessments, strengths inventories and mindfulness.

  — [Simply Psychology summary](https://www.simplypsychology.org/adhd-coaching-intervention.html); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/377890776_Development_of_a_Manualized_Coaching_Intervention_for_Adult_ADHD)
- **Research protocols:**
  - Prevatt & Yelland used an 8-week program.
  - Kubik used group coaching of six 2-hour sessions plus a 1-month follow-up.

  — [Prevatt & Yelland 2015](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1087054713480036); [Kubik 2010 summary](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/24191479_Efficacy_of_ADHD_coaching_for_adults_with_ADHD)
- **Progress measures:**
  - The ADHD Benefits of Coaching Scale (ABCS; Deal, Sleeper-Triplett et al., 2015, *Journal of Attention Disorders*) is a coaching-specific outcome measure. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/1087054714558118)
  - BDEFS has been validated in a large multisite college sample. — [PMC8010583](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8010583/)
  - Kubik had clients rate 22 areas of concern before and after coaching. — [Kubik 2010](https://doi.org/10.1177/1087054708329960)

### Inferences
- **App blueprint:**
  - **Onboarding:** a guided intake using the APA question list (as conversation, not a form), producing three long-term goals and reminder preferences. Include a light-touch risk screen modelled on the OQ-45 critical items, with escalation.
  - **Weekly review ritual:** wins, then what helped and what hindered, then 1–3 goals for next week, each with steps and if-then plans.
  - **Daily touchpoints:** a morning plan and an evening review, plus book-ends around hard tasks.
  - **Periodic re-rating:** a BDEFS-style self-rating or ABCS-style benefit rating every 8–12 weeks, mirroring the end-of-contract evaluation.

### Gaps
- I found no authoritative data on typical between-session check-in frequency (daily vs 2–3 per week). Sources say only "at regular intervals or as needed".
- I could not access the full text of the 2026 Ahmann, Saviet & Otto prospective study (HTTP 403), so its session cadence is unknown.

## Evidence base for ADHD coaching outcomes, and what drives benefit

### Takeaway
The evidence is consistently positive but methodologically weak:
- Of 19 outcome studies, 18 reported EF and symptom gains, though most were small and uncontrolled.
- There are only about two RCTs, and neither was in working adults.
- The strongest data come from US college students (Edge/Field et al., Prevatt & Yelland).
- Newer adult prospective studies (Ahmann, Saviet & Otto, 2026) report gains maintained at 6 weeks.
- UK NICE NG87 acknowledges coaching as an adjunct but treats structured psychological intervention and CBT as core.

The proposed active ingredients are an ADHD-informed alliance, psychoeducation, concrete goal-setting, skills practice and accountability.

### Cited Findings
- **Ahmann et al. (2018) descriptive review:**
  - 22 studies, of which 19 examined outcomes. Designs ranged from case studies to RCTs, across ages from elementary school to adults. 16 studied individual coaching and 3 studied groups.
  - Results: 18 of 19 found improved ADHD and EF symptoms; 6 found improved well-being; 3 found gains maintained; 6 found high satisfaction.

  — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/326098410_A_Descriptive_Review_of_ADHD_Coaching_Research_Implications_for_College_Students); [ERIC full text](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1182373.pdf)
- A CHADD conference slide summarising the evidence notes most studies had n ≤ 10 and most lacked control groups. — [CHADD 2018 slides](https://chadd.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/2B_Emerging_Evidence_for_the_Effectiveness_of_ADHD_Coaching_.pdf)
- **Australian ADHD Guideline:**
  - "High-quality evidence is lacking." It identified two RCTs:
    - **Field et al. (2013):** college students, 88 coached vs 39 controls. Significantly higher LASSI (Learning and Study Strategies Inventory) total and all subscales.
    - **Evans et al. (2014):** teens, 24 coached vs 12 controls. Only parent-rated family functioning was better than control.
  - It names three core coaching components: a collaborative, client-centred, client-driven process; ADHD education with tools; and skills coaching.

  — [AADPA Guideline](https://adhdguideline.aadpa.com.au/non-pharmacological/adhd-coaching/)
- **Prevatt & Yelland (2015, *Journal of Attention Disorders*):**
  - 148 college students over 5 years, 8-week coaching combining CBT and psychoeducation, framed around EF.
  - Significant improvement in all 10 LASSI areas, self-esteem, symptom distress, and satisfaction with school and work.
  - Results were consistent across semesters and across novice coaches.

  — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1087054713480036)
- **Edge Foundation / Wayne State:**
  - A US$1M, 2-year study completed in 2010, with randomly selected experimental and control groups.
  - Edge reports effect sizes for self-regulation "more than double the typical educational intervention, and executive functioning… quadruple".
  - This is the funder's own description. The peer-reviewed version is Field et al. 2013.

  — [Edge Foundation research](https://edgefoundation.org/individual-coaching-old/coaching-research/)
- **Kubik (2010):**
  - 45 adults (30 women, 15 men) in group coaching with psychoeducation (6 × 2 h plus a 1-month follow-up).
  - "Strong impact" on cognition, distractibility, inattention and social/behavioural outcomes, with gains maintained over 1–4 years.
  - Observational, with no control group.

  — [Kubik 2010, J Atten Disord](https://doi.org/10.1177/1087054708329960); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19276311/)
- **Ahmann, Saviet & Otto (2026):** two papers in *American Journal of Lifestyle Medicine* (DOI prefix 10.1177/1559827626…): "Coaching for Adults With ADHD: A Prospective Study" and "…Gains at a Six-Week Follow-Up". I could not access the full text (403), so their sample sizes and effect sizes are unknown. — [Prospective study](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/15598276261432960); [6-week follow-up](https://doi.org/10.1177/15598276261473877)
- A Journal of Postsecondary Education and Disability (2024) paper on college coaching exists. — [ERIC EJ1445898](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1445898.pdf) (not fetched)
- A paper on potential adverse events of ADHD college coaching exists ("ADHD College Coaching: Potential Adverse Events", *ADHD Report* 2018). I saw only the title. — [Guilford](https://guilfordjournals.com/doi/10.1521/adhd.2018.26.2.10)
- **CHADD summary of outcomes:** coached college students develop better EF and self-determination, take more responsibility, use goal-attainment skills more, regulate emotion better and expect to perform better. Group coaching shows gains in anxiety, planning, self-efficacy and time management. In a large-scale study, coached first-year students had better retention and graduation rates. — [CHADD/help4adhd PDF](https://chadd.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/coaching-adults.pdf)
- **UK NICE NG87:** coaching "parallel[s] a mentoring paradigm by supporting people with ADHD to rehearse newly learned skills on a daily basis" and has been used as an adjunct to group CBT programmes. "Formal studies of the effectiveness of psychotherapy and coaching have not yet been carried out, but many adults… report that they gain benefit." When a non-drug treatment is indicated, NICE recommends a structured supportive psychological intervention focused on ADHD, with regular follow-up in person or by phone. — [NICE NG87 recommendations](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng87/chapter/recommendations); [NICE full guideline](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng87/evidence/full-guideline-pdf-4783651311) (the coaching quote is from the full guideline via search snippet)
- **Barkley:** medication is the only treatment shown to normalise EF, and only while active. Psychosocial help works through accommodations at the point of performance. — [Barkley fact sheet](https://www.russellbarkley.org/factsheets/ADHD_EF_and_SR.pdf)

### Inferences
- Likely active ingredients across sources:
  - an ADHD-informed, shame-free alliance
  - psychoeducation about one's own patterns
  - concrete, collaboratively set goals broken into small steps
  - in-session action plus between-session accountability (which matches "point of performance")
  - CBT-style planning and if-then plans
  - strengths focus
- An AI coach could plausibly deliver most of these, especially the high-frequency accountability that human coaches find costly.
- An AI cannot easily replicate the human relationship. Body-doubling and robot-companion studies hint that a non-human presence may give partial benefit, but this is speculative.
- Claims of efficacy for an app should be modest. The field's evidence is mainly pre/post designs with student samples.

### Gaps
- There is no meta-analytic effect size for adult ADHD coaching. I found no RCT in employed adults.
- I found no studies of AI or chatbot ADHD coaching outcomes, beyond the CBT-informed app RCT noted earlier, which I did not verify.
- The 2026 Ahmann studies' details are unknown.

## UK context

### Takeaway
In the UK, most funded ADHD coaching comes through the DWP's **Access to Work** grant as workplace strategy or neurodiversity coaching, which is practical, job-focused and EF-oriented. NICE NG87 treats coaching as an adjunct with weak formal evidence.

### Cited Findings
- Access to Work is a DWP grant for disabled and neurodivergent people in work. It funds coaching, training and assistive technology. ADHD coaching may appear in reports as "ADHD coaching, neurodivergence coaching, support worker, support strategy coaching". — [ND Transformative Coaching](https://www.ndtransformativecoaching.co.uk/access-to-work-coaching/); [Genius Within](https://geniuswithin.org/individuals/in-work/access-to-work/)
- Workplace strategy coaching covers time management, organisation, working memory, communication, and stress or performance concerns. — [ADHD Centre: Access to Work for ADHD](https://www.adhdcentre.co.uk/access-to-work-for-adhd/)
- The RCPsych report CR235 on adults with ADHD gives good-practice guidance. Its content was not reviewed here. — [RCPsych CR235](https://www.rcpsych.ac.uk/docs/default-source/improving-care/better-mh-policy/college-reports/cr235-adhd-in-adults---good-practice-guidance.pdf)

## Referral, scope of practice and red flags

### Takeaway
Coaches refer out, or work alongside clinicians, in these situations:
- untreated comorbid psychiatric conditions (depression, bipolar, anxiety, substance misuse, personality disorder)
- risk indicators (hopelessness, worthlessness, suicidal thoughts, intrusive thoughts, anger they fear acting on)
- major life stressors (divorce, bereavement, marital crisis)
- serious physical illness
- medication problems
- persistent non-engagement despite support
- deep self-esteem, trauma or emotional-crisis work

Coaches never diagnose or treat.

### Cited Findings
- **CHADD's referral triggers:**
  - the client hasn't applied strategies despite the coach's resources and reminders
  - an untreated co-existing psychiatric condition (depression, bipolar disorder, anxiety, substance abuse, personality disorder)
  - stressful life circumstances (marital problems, divorce, death of a loved one)
  - serious physical or chronic illness

  In these cases the coach works collaboratively with clinicians, or may end coaching. "If assistance is needed with emotional, psychiatric, or interpersonal problems, then a licensed mental health professional should be consulted." — [CHADD/help4adhd PDF](https://chadd.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/coaching-adults.pdf)
- **APA intake risk screen:** OQ-45 items scored "almost always" are flagged for worthlessness, hopelessness, intrusive thoughts, anger they might act on, and suicidal thoughts. Intake also asks about medication problems, sleep and eating. — [APA Intake Interview PDF](https://www.apa.org/pubs/books/supplemental/ADHD-Coaching/The_Intake_Interview.pdf)
- **Scope limits and consumer red flags:**
  - Coaches are "not licensed to assess, diagnose, or treat ADHD or any other condition".
  - They should not address "deep-seated self-esteem issues", heal past trauma or "fix" people in emotional crisis.
  - Red flags in a coach: promising quick fixes or cures, implying they can diagnose, guaranteeing results, being vague about training or method, and pressuring clients into long contracts.
  - These come from practitioner and consumer sites, not standards bodies. — [Science Works Health](https://www.scienceworkshealth.com/post/how-to-choose-executive-function-coach); [Kontext guide for therapists](https://www.kontext.coach/therapists); [Lightbulb ADHD](https://lightbulbadhd.com/blog/how-to-find-qualified-adhd-coach/)
- ADDitude advises referral to doctors when a client struggles to function in specific situations and may need diagnosis or a medication review. — [ADDitude](https://www.additudemag.com/adhd-therapy-cbt-vs-coaching/)
- Coaching complements medication and ADHD-focused CBT in a "three-pronged multimodal approach". — [CHADD: Attention Monthly](https://chadd.org/adhd-news/adhd-news-adults/attention-monthly-evidence-based-coaching-for-adult-adhd/)

### Inferences
- **Hard escalation rules for an AI coach:**
  - Detect hopelessness, worthlessness, self-harm or suicidal language and anger-risk language. Respond with a safety response and UK resources (for example, Samaritans 116 123, NHS 111 option 2 for urgent mental health, 999).
  - Prompt a GP or prescriber for medication side effects or forgotten doses, sleep or eating problems, or a suspected comorbidity.
  - Gently refer to a therapist when sessions turn repeatedly to trauma or deep shame.
  - After N weeks of consistent non-follow-through, suggest a clinician or medication review instead of increasing pressure.
- The AI should state its scope plainly (it does not diagnose or treat) and avoid claims of guaranteed results.

### Gaps
- PAAC's and ICF's formal ethics-code wording on referral was not retrieved.
- No source gave a standard numeric threshold for "non-engagement" referral.
